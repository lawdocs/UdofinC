## Findings

### F1. Advocates: statute, BCI Rules and enforcement (2024–2026)

#### F1.1 Statutory frame — Advocates Act 1961 [V: 1]
- **s.29.** From the appointed day there is "only one class of persons entitled to practise the profession of law", namely advocates.
- **s.30.** An advocate on a State roll may practise as of right in all courts, including the Supreme Court, and before any tribunal or person legally authorised to take evidence. This is subject to the Act.
- **s.33.** No one who is not enrolled may practise in any court or before any authority, except as other laws provide.
- **s.35(1).** Where a State Bar Council has reason to believe an advocate on its roll is guilty of professional or other misconduct, it must refer the case to its disciplinary committee. Disciplinary jurisdiction covers advocates, not platforms.
- **s.45.** Practising when not entitled is punishable with imprisonment of up to six months.
- **s.45A (touts).** Inserted by the Advocates (Amendment) Act 2023 (Act 33 of 2023). The India Code note gives effect from 30 Sep 2024. The section text was not reproduced in the fetched extract **[K]**.
- **s.49(1)(c) and s.49(1)(ah).** These give the BCI power to make rules on standards of professional conduct and etiquette, and on the conditions of the right to practise.
- **s.17.** Each State Bar Council must "prepare and maintain" a roll containing names and addresses. s.19 requires an authenticated copy to be sent to the BCI. **Neither section requires the roll to be published.** This matters for DPDP s.3(c)(ii)(B) (F4.2).

What this means for Equator:
- A platform is not itself subject to BCI discipline.
- The platform's practical exposure is:
  1. advocates cannot lawfully take part, so the product fails;
  2. court directions, including the denial of IT Act s.79 safe harbour (F1.4);
  3. BCI and State Bar Council complaints and takedown demands;
  4. s.45 or s.45A exposure if Equator itself "sells legal services" or acts as a tout.
- Practice of law includes non-litigious work (*BCI v A.K. Balaji* (2018) 5 SCC 379, as cited in pack Appendix O X-01 [45]).

#### F1.3 Cases and BCI actions
1. **Madras HC — *P.N. Vignesh v Chairman and Members, Bar Council of India*, 3 Jul 2024 (S.M. Subramaniam and C. Kumarappan JJ; reported as 2024 MHC 2515)** [S: 2, 3]:
   - Held that platforms offering "online lawyer services" were in effect selling legal services, in breach of r.36 (touting).
   - Held that such intermediaries could not rely on IT Act s.79.
   - Directed the BCI to frame guidelines and have State Bar Councils start disciplinary action against advocates who advertise or solicit work through intermediaries.
   - Stated that "the legal profession is not and can never be treated as a business".
2. **BCI press release, July 2024** [S: 2]:
   - Cease-and-desist notices to Quikr India, Sulekha.com, Just Dial and Grotal.com.
   - Illegal legal-service advertisements to be removed within four weeks.
   - State Bar Councils to report action taken by 10 Aug 2024; compliance hearing on 20 Sep 2024.
3. **Supreme Court — *Sulekha.com New Media Pvt Ltd v P.N. Vignesh*, SLP (C) 25794/2024** [S: 3]:
   - Notice issued to the BCI on about 13 Nov 2024 by Hrishikesh Roy and S.V.N. Bhatti JJ.
   - To be tagged with SLP (C) 17844/2024.
   - **No stay was reported.**
   - The question is whether third-party platforms that aggregate lawyer profiles breach r.36 *even when they publish only information the BCI Rules permit*. This is exactly the question for an Equator advocate directory.
   - In April 2025 the appeal was reported as still pending [S: 6]. I found no decision up to 23 Oct 2026 **[K]**.
   - Until the Supreme Court decides, the Madras HC judgment stands and is the most specific Indian authority.
4. **BCI press release, 17 Mar 2025** (after a law firm's celebrity promotional video) [S: 5, 6]:
   - Condemned advertising of legal services through social media, promotional videos, influencer and celebrity endorsements, and event banners.
   - Asked digital platforms to vet legal content and remove violating material.
   - Threatened suspension or cancellation of enrolment, and referral for contempt.
5. **Supreme Court — *Anil Pandey v BCI*, WP (C) 817/2026** [S: 4]:
   - Notice issued about 14 Jul 2026 by Surya Kant CJI, Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana JJ, returnable 15 Sep 2026.
   - Seeks enforcement of r.36 against digital solicitation, reels, influencer collaborations and monetised content.
   - Seeks a "Code of Digital Professional Conduct" under Advocates Act s.49.
   - No later order was found **[K]**. A digital-conduct code could follow and could expressly cover platform listings.
6. **Allahabad HC (2018)** issued notice on a petition by an advocate against lawyer-listing portals, citing rr.36 and 37 [S: 12]. No outcome was found.
7. **Delhi HC, 2 Sep 2026 — *Aditya Chauhan v Bar Council of Delhi*, WP (C) 12711/2026** (Upadhyaya CJ and Tejas Karia J) [S: 38]:
   - The BCD "Enrolment Status" portal shows residential address, mobile number, date of birth and parents' names to anyone who enters an enrolment number and year, with no login or CAPTCHA.
   - The petitioner argued that ss.17 and 19 do not authorise this disclosure.
   - The petition was disposed of as a representation, to be decided within eight weeks (about end-October/November 2026). The outcome is unknown **[K]**.
8. **Consumer law** — *Bar of Indian Lawyers v D.K. Gandhi PS National Institute of Communicable Diseases* (SC, May 2024; CA 2646–2649/2009) [S: 13]:
   - Advocates' services fall outside "service" under the Consumer Protection Acts 1986 and 2019, as a contract "of" personal service.
   - This removes consumer-forum liability *of the advocate*. It does **not** relax r.36, and it does not exempt Equator's own platform services (F3.1).

#### F1.4 Can a platform do (a)–(e) for advocates? (analysis; counsel to sign off)
| # | Model | Position | Basis | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | **Opt-in listing of advocates** | *Arguable but unsettled.* The safest form is an advocate-initiated, opt-in profile limited to the five Schedule items, shown in uniform format with neutral (alphabetical or random) order, no SEO landing pages, no "hire now" calls to action and no fee display. "Featured", "top" and "recommended" markers go beyond the Schedule. This matches and supports the pack's current position:

- Appendix O X-11: non-cash credits only.

#### F1.5 Chartered accountants, company secretaries, cost accountants (brief)
- **CAs** [V: 14; S: 15]:
  - Clauses (6) (solicitation) and (7) (advertisement) of Part I of the First Schedule to the CA Act 1949 apply. Breaching Council guidelines is misconduct under Clause (1), Part II, Second Schedule.
  - **ICAI Ethical Standards Board, 23 Oct 2019:** it is "not permissible" for members to list themselves on app-based service-provider aggregators. Online directory or trade-directory entries are allowed only under conditions: listed under "Chartered Accountants", normal type, alphabetical order, no undue prominence.
  - **ICAI Website Advisory, 14 Oct 2020**, prohibits on websites:
    - fees, or saying a service is free;
    - client names and logos;
    - testimonials naming clients;
    - grading or "tier" claims, and awards;
    - subjective superiority claims;
    - "why choose us" features;
    - push-model services.
  - The fee-sharing and commission clauses (Part I Clauses (2)–(3)) were not opened this round **[K]**.
  - **Consequence:** a marketplace-style listing of CAs is likely to be treated as an "aggregator". At most, allow a uniform, alphabetical, verification-only entry, and only after an ICAI opinion.
- **Company secretaries** [V: 16]:
  - The ICSI (Guidelines for Advertisement by Company Secretaries) 2020 treat websites as advertisement (Clause 4.3).
  - Content is limited to a write-up of services the PCS may lawfully render plus firm particulars.
  - Violations are misconduct under Chapter V of the CS Act 1980.
  - The FAQ does not address aggregators, paid listings, fees or testimonials **[K]**. Apply the ICAI-equivalent restrictions by default **[R]**.
- **Cost accountants** [S: 17]:
  - The ICMAI Council Guidelines for Advertisement 2025 (reported March 2026; effective on Gazette notification, date not confirmed **[K]**):
    - prohibit firms from **listing services on aggregator websites**;
    - prohibit membership of paid or unpaid referral networks;
    - prohibit mentioning fees (unless a regulator requires it);
    - bar "expert" and "specialist" claims;
    - require client consent before naming clients.
  - Third-party platforms may show only a bare "cost accountant" statement, without contact details.

**Bottom line for professionals:**
- Paid placement, ratings, fee display and "featured" status are **OFF for advocates, CAs, CSs and CMAs** in any public directory.
- The safest lawful form for the cross-group use case (execution, registration and NPA recovery) is a **client-side panel register inside each lender's tenant**, not a public marketplace.

### F2. Non-advocate neutrals: advertising, Mediation Act 2023 and appointment integrity

#### F2.1 Is there a law restricting advertising by non-advocate arbitrators or mediators?
- **No Indian statute was found that specifically restricts advertising by arbitrators or mediators as such.** The general law applies to them as to any service provider:
  - Consumer Protection Act 2019 s.2(28) (misleading advertisement);
  - the CCPA Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements Guidelines 2022 [S: 29];
  - the Dark Patterns Guidelines 2023 [S: 28];
  - the E-Commerce Rules where Equator is the marketplace (F3).
- **The neutral's own profession still governs them:**
  - An **advocate-neutral** carries r.36 everywhere. Whether an institution-run *neutral* profile counts as the advocate's "advertising" is untested (pack OI-03; J-032/J-083 [44]).
  - A **CA, CS or CMA neutral** carries the institute rules in F1.5.
  - Retired judges follow no codified advertising rule that I found **[K]**. Institutional practice is restraint.
- **Mediation Act 2023:**
  - The Mediation Council of India was established by notification of 27 Aug 2026 under s.31(1), head office in Delhi [S: 18, 19]. No appointments, regulations or code of conduct were reported as of those articles **[K]**.
  - Under **s.38**, the Council must:
    - "lay down standards for professional and ethical conduct of mediators under sub-section (3) of section 15" (s.38(f));
    - provide for registration of mediators (s.38(e));
    - recognise mediation institutes and mediation service providers (s.38(i), (j));
    - "lay down standards for professional and ethical conduct of the mediation institutes and mediation service providers" (s.38(l)) [S: 20].
  - **s.41** requires mediation service providers to "accredit mediators and maintain panel of mediators" (s.41(a)) and "promote professional and ethical conduct amongst mediators" (s.41(d)) [S: 21].
  - **Watch item:** MCI conduct standards for mediators and MSPs could regulate advertising and paid visibility. If Equator seeks MSP recognition, a paid-visibility scheme inside its own panel would sit uneasily with s.41(a) and (d).
- **Comparator codes (not Indian law):**
  - The South Carolina *Standards of Conduct for Mediators*: Std VII requires truthful public communications about services and credentials and "promises and guarantees of results" are to be avoided; the Std VIII comment says a mediator "should not accept a fee for referral of a matter to another mediator or to any other person" [V: 23].
  - The IAMA *Principles of Conduct for Mediators* 2003, Principle 7 (truthful advertising and solicitation; no guarantees of results) and Principle 8 (full fee disclosure) [V: 24].
  - The IMI Code was not opened this round **[K]**.

#### F2.2 Appointment integrity when neutrals pay for visibility
- **Statute (not re-opened this round; standard provisions):**
  - A&C Act 1996 s.11 (appointment; s.11(3A) designated arbitral institutions);
  - s.12(1) and the Fifth/Sixth Schedules (disclosure);
  - s.12(3) (justifiable doubts as to independence or impartiality);
  - s.12(5) and the Seventh Schedule (ineligibility);
  - s.18 (equal treatment of parties).
- ***Central Organisation for Railway Electrification v ECI-SPIC-SMO-MCML (JV)***, Constitution Bench, 8 Nov 2024 [S: 22]:
  - The majority (Chandrachud CJI; Roy and Narasimha JJ partly dissenting) held that the s.18 equality principle "applies at all stages … including at the stage of appointment of arbitrators".
  - Unilateral appointment clauses in public-private contracts are invalid.
  - Counterbalancing through a **curated panel** (as in *Voestalpine*) does not cure the problem.
  - The ruling applies prospectively.
- **Consequences for Equator's design [R]:**
  1. Equator's appointment and allocation must be demonstrably free of commercial influence. A fee paid by a neutral to the appointing institution is a commercial relationship between the neutral and the appointer. A party can argue that the institution's "curated" list is shaped by who pays. That is a s.12(3) or *CORE*-type challenge to the award, not just a reputational issue.
  2. Group structure aggravates the risk. Infrabyx group companies (Finbyx: LOS/LMS vendor to NBFCs; Udofin: a digital lending platform) serve lenders who will be repeat claimants in Equator NPA matters. A neutral who pays Equator, in a case where the claimant is a client of an Equator group company, invites an apparent-bias argument. NN-16 already requires disclosure of paid commercial relationships to counterparties. The group relationship must be disclosed too.
  3. The pack's `NO_PAID_PLACEMENT` firewall (Appendix N §2.5, §6) is the right control and must stay **absolute for Equator-administered matters.**
  4. Paid "preference" can exist, if at all, **only** where the *party* chooses a *private-practice* neutral for a *non-administered* engagement (Chambers or Marketplace booking). Even then it must be labelled, sit in a separate slot, be offered on equal terms, and never be readable by allocation.
- **How institutions avoid the conflict:**
  - Pack research S9 E-1 (no empanelment fee) and Appendix O X-04 reflect the common institutional model: no listing fees and published allocation rules.
  - Codes treat referral fees as improper [23].
  - I found **no** Indian or major international institution that sells visibility on its own appointment panel **[K: not exhaustively searched]**.

### F3. Marketplace law

#### F3.1 Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020, as amended in 2026
- **Coverage.**
  - The Rules apply to "all e-commerce transactions involving goods and services (including digital products)" [S: 27].
  - A **marketplace e-commerce entity** is one that "provides an information technology platform on a digital or electronic network" to facilitate transactions between buyers and sellers [S: 27].
  - A services marketplace where parties find and book neutrals, chambers, trainers or experts and pay through Equator is, on its face, a marketplace. Equator is the marketplace e-commerce entity and the neutrals or chambers are "sellers".
  - Caveats:
    1. After *D.K. Gandhi* [13], an advocate's services are not a consumer "service", so the Rules may not bite on advocate engagements. The case did not decide whether arbitral or mediator services are a contract "of" personal service **[K]**.
    2. Equator's *own* platform services (subscriptions, administration fees, Explorer reviews) are services sold online, whatever the neutral's status.
  - **Default [R]:** comply in full for every marketplace surface.
- **Duties already in force (2020 Rules)** [S: 25, 26, 27; P: pack Appendix O §6.3]:
  - r.4: grievance officer; acknowledgement within 48 hours and redress within one month (r.4(5)); no unfair trade practice; no pre-ticked consent boxes (r.4(9)); no price manipulation.
  - r.5(1): marketplace compliance with IT Act s.79 and the intermediary rules.
  - Seller undertaking that descriptions and images are accurate.
  - r.5(3)(a): seller details shown before purchase (name, registration status, address, customer care, any rating or aggregated feedback).
  - r.5(3)(f): **an explanation of the main parameters that determine ranking of goods or sellers and their relative importance**, in plain, publicly available language.
  - A description of any differentiated treatment of sellers (sub-rule number not confirmed **[K]**).
- **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules 2026** — notified 9 Sep 2026 (Gazette CG-DL-E-10092026-276125); **commencement 1 Jan 2027** per AZB [25]; IndiaLaw does not state the date [26] **[K]**. Key changes:
  - "Ranking" now covers sellers as well as goods and services. **Main parameters must be listed in descending order of significance** (r.3(1)(j), r.5(3)(f)).
  - **New r.4(11)(c):** no manipulation of search results or indexes that misleads users given their query.
  - **New r.4(12):** **sponsored listings must be clearly and prominently identified.**
  - **New r.4(15):** annual self-audit for dark patterns, with a compliance certificate displayed prominently. This makes the CCPA advisory of 5 Jun 2025 binding.
  - r.4(5): the complainant gets a copy of the complaint as recorded. r.4(7): mandatory National Consumer Helpline convergence.
  - r.5(3)(a): seller website and e-mail are also displayed where available.
  - **New r.5(6):** no using platform data to favour sellers sharing the marketplace's brand or name, related or not. No presenting a seller as associated with the marketplace without the consumer's express, affirmative consent. *This reaches any "Equator"-branded or group-affiliated neutral, chambers or the Equator ADR Institute shown in the marketplace.*
  - **New r.5(7):** no fees bundled with unrelated services (loyalty and membership carve-out).
  - New r.6(5)(j): sellers provide any government identification number (GSTIN, MSME) for display.

#### F3.2 CCPA guidance
- **Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements for Misleading Advertisements 2022** (notified 9 Jun 2022) [S: 29]:
  - Advertisements must be truthful and substantiated.
  - Endorsements must reflect the endorser's genuine and reasonably current opinion.
  - **Material connections must be disclosed.**
  - Disclaimers may not contradict the claim or hide its commercial intent.
  - "Top mediator", "95% settlement rate" or "most trusted" claims about neutrals would need substantiation. They also collide with NN-28.
- **Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns 2023** (notified and in force 30 Nov 2023) [S: 28]:
  - Apply to all platforms offering goods or services in India, and to advertisers and sellers.
  - List 12 patterns, including false urgency, basket sneaking, confirm shaming, forced action, subscription trap, interface interference, bait and switch, drip pricing, **disguised advertisement**, nagging, trick question and rogue malware.
  - A paid neutral placement that looks like an organic result or an editorial recommendation is a disguised advertisement.
  - The pack already maps this as DP-09 with `DARK_PATTERN_label_sponsored` (Appendix O §6) [45].

#### F3.3 IT Act and IT Rules 2021 (intermediary)
- **Intermediary role.** For user-generated content on the marketplace (neutral bios, reviews, chambers pages), Equator is an intermediary. It must follow IT Rules 2021 r.3 due diligence (terms, prohibited-content notice, grievance officer).
- **2026 amendment.** The amendment of 10 Feb 2026 (G.S.R. 120(E), in force 20 Feb 2026) brought **7-day grievance disposal** and **3-hour takedown** for specified content [P: 44 A8].
- **No safe harbour for advocate listings.** *Vignesh* denied s.79 safe harbour to lawyer-listing intermediaries [S: 3]. Equator should not plan on s.79 protection for advocate listings.
- **Reviews (not re-verified this round).** IS 19000:2022 on online consumer reviews (pack N, CN-05) is a voluntary BIS standard **[K]**. Review moderation, no paid or incentivised reviews, and the right of reply are already in Appendix N §7.

#### F3.4 Tax on the marketplace
- **GST: what Equator itself charges.**
  - Equator's marketplace take and listing fees (Appendix O O-R2: 15%, 12% or 10%) are Equator's own taxable supply to the neutral or chambers, invoiced with GST.
  - Rate and SAC were not confirmed this round **[K: tax adviser]**.
- **GST s.9(5) (operator pays tax for notified services).** The notified list (Notification 17/2017-CT(Rate), as amended: passenger transport, accommodation, housekeeping, restaurant) does not include legal, arbitral or mediation services. **Not re-opened this round [K]**, so treat s.9(5) as **not applicable**.
- **GST s.52 TCS** [V: 30; S: 31]:
  - An e-commerce operator (CGST s.2(45)) that **collects the consideration** for suppliers' supplies must collect TCS. Registration is compulsory irrespective of turnover (s.24(x); FAQ Q5).
  - The rate is **0.5% (0.25% CGST + 0.25% SGST/UTGST, or 0.5% IGST) from 10 Jul 2024** (Notification 15/2024-CT); it was 1% before.
  - **TCS is not collected on exempt supplies (FAQ Q14), nor on supplies where the recipient pays tax under reverse charge (FAQ Q15).**
  - Monthly GSTR-8 applies.
- **RCM for professional supplies** [V: 32; S: 33]:
  - Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate) entry 2: legal services by an individual advocate, senior advocate or firm of advocates to a business entity → RCM.
  - Entry 3: services of an **arbitral tribunal** to a business entity → RCM.
  - Supplies of legal services to individuals and to non-business or small-business recipients are exempt under Notification 12/2017-CT(Rate) **[K: entry not opened]**.
  - Mediators and conciliators have no RCM entry, so they charge forward GST if registered **[K]**.
  - Practical result: for a lender (business entity) booking an arbitrator through Equator, the tribunal's fee is RCM and needs no TCS. Equator's own fee is forward-charged. Mediator fees collected by Equator for GST-registered mediators attract TCS.
- **Income-tax Act 2025** [S: 34]:
  - The successor to s.194-O is **s.393(1), Table Sl. No. 8(v)**, effective 1 Apr 2026.
  - Rate: 0.1% TDS on the gross amount of sales or services facilitated by an e-commerce operator, at the earlier of credit or payment.
  - Resident individual or HUF participants are excluded up to ₹5 lakh a year if PAN or Aadhaar is furnished. There is no threshold for others. Professional services are covered.
  - Not confirmed **[K: tax adviser]**: how this interacts with professional-fee TDS deducted by the business client, and whether a pure "listing without payment collection" model falls outside.

#### F3.5 Competition Act 2002 (note only)
- Risk is minor at launch.
- If Equator gains a strong position in lender ODR, watch for:
  - tying marketplace use to administered services, or vice versa (s.4(2)(d) **[K]**);
  - self-preferencing of group-affiliated neutrals or chambers. This is better handled now under E-Commerce r.5(6).

### F4. Data protection for a professional directory

#### F4.1 Commencement
- The DPDP Act's substantive duties (ss.3–17 and others) commence on **13 May 2027** (G.S.R. 843(E); Rules G.S.R. 846(E), both 13 Nov 2025) [P: 44 A8].
- Until then, IT Act s.43A and the SPDI Rules apply.
- A directory launched in 2026–27 will hold data that becomes DPDP-governed mid-life. Design to DPDP now **[R]**.

#### F4.2 The s.3(c)(ii) "publicly available" exemption and its limits
- **Text** [S: 35]. The Act does not apply to personal data "made or caused to be made publicly available by (A) the Data Principal to whom such personal data relates; or (B) any other person who is under an obligation under any law for the time being in force in India to make such personal data publicly available". The illustration is a blogger who posts her own data on social media.
- **Limits that matter for a directory [R; counsel to confirm]:**
  1. **Scope is the data as published.** Data an advocate publishes on her own website (limb A) is outside the Act. Once Equator combines it with non-public data, the combined record contains in-scope personal data. Non-public data here includes verification evidence, KYC, phone numbers obtained from forms, engagement history, tenant notes, and the fact that a lender empanels her.
  2. **Bar Council rolls are not clearly limb (B).**
     - Advocates Act s.17 requires a roll to be "prepared and maintained", with names and addresses. Neither s.17 nor s.19 requires publication [V: 1].
     - The Delhi HC petition challenges online disclosure of mobile numbers, dates of birth and parents' names precisely because no law requires it [S: 38].
     - Roll-portal data is therefore not safely "publicly available under a legal obligation". Fields beyond name and address are the least defensible.
  3. **The exemption is fragile in time.** If the person later removes the data, or the Bar Council masks it (a possible outcome of the BCD representation), the basis falls away. The copy Equator holds is then plainly in scope.
  4. **It removes only the DPDP Act.** IT Act s.43(b) (unauthorised downloading or extraction of data from a computer resource; not re-opened this round **[K]**), portal terms, r.36, the CPA and the E-Commerce Rules still apply.
     - Prior research found **no State Bar Council that publishes an API or terms permitting automated access** [P: 44 J-030/J-076/J-081].
     - Scripted querying of roll portals is scraping in substance. Rolls are not licensed for reuse.
- **Conclusion [R]:** do not build the directory on s.3(c)(ii). Build it on **consent (s.6) after notice (s.5)**, with profiles created or claimed by the professional. Use **staff-assisted verification against the official portal** (screenshot, URL, timestamp, two-person review), as pack Appendix S §4.5 already says. Do **no bulk import** of roll data.

#### F4.3 Consent, legitimate use, accuracy, rights
- **Consent and legitimate use.**
  - Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, by clear affirmative action, and limited to the data necessary for the purpose (s.6, standard text; not re-opened **[K]**).
  - The pack's per-field display consents (Appendix N §2.3 step 7) satisfy this.
  - s.7(a) (voluntarily provided for a specified purpose) [S: 36] covers using an advocate's details *in the case in which she appears*. It does **not** cover reusing them for a directory or marketing.
- **Accuracy and freshness.** Under s.8(3), where personal data is likely to be used for a decision affecting the Data Principal, **or disclosed to another Data Fiduciary**, the fiduciary must ensure completeness, accuracy and consistency [S: 37]. Two consequences:
  1. Every directory record shared with Finbyx/Udofin tenants needs `verified_at`, `source` and a re-verification clock.
  2. A stale enrolment or a lapsed CoP is a compliance defect, not just a UX defect.
- **Processors.** Under s.8(2), processors may be engaged "only under a valid contract". Under s.8(1), the fiduciary stays responsible "irrespective of any agreement to the contrary" [S: 37].
- **Erasure.** Under s.8(7), data must be erased on withdrawal of consent or when the purpose is no longer served, *including by processors* [S: 37].
- **Rights.** Data Principals have the rights of access (s.11), correction, completion, updating and erasure (s.12) and grievance redressal (s.13) (standard text; not re-opened **[K]**). The profile editor and a "remove me" flow must work everywhere the record has been shared.

#### F4.4 Same directory across group companies (Finbyx, Udofin)
- **Separate fiduciaries.** Each group company is a separate legal person and a separate Data Fiduciary. **The DPDP Act has no intra-group exemption or "legitimate interests" ground** (s.7 lists specific legitimate uses only; none covers group sharing).
- **Sharing is a disclosure.** Making Equator's directory available to Finbyx/Udofin, or their lender clients, is a disclosure to another fiduciary for a new purpose: engagement for execution, registration or recovery work. It therefore needs either:
  1. consent that names the recipients and the purpose at collection, for example: "Make my professional profile available to lender clients of Infrabyx group companies Finbyx and Udofin, for engaging me in execution, registration and recovery matters" (separate toggle, revocable); **or**
  2. a structure in which a single group entity is the directory's fiduciary and the others act only as its processors. This does not fit if the lenders use the data for their own engagements.
- **Recommended role model [R]:**
  - **Directory Owner** (one named entity; Equator, or a group shared-services company) — fiduciary for collection, verification and publication.
  - **Each consuming company and each lender tenant** — an independent fiduciary for its own engagement use, receiving data under a **Group Data-Sharing Agreement (GDSA)** or tenant terms.
  - **Hosting / IT operator** — processor under s.8(2) contracts.
- **NN-16 extension [R].** Neutral data, allocation data and case data never flow to Finbyx/Udofin. Lender-side panel-advocate performance data never flows back into the neutral directory or allocation.

### F5. Group companies sharing employees and operational data

#### F5.1 DPDP and employee data across group entities
- **Who can rely on s.7(i).** s.7(i) permits processing "for the purposes of employment or those related to safeguarding the employer from loss or liability", and for services or benefits an employee asks for [S: 36]. It is the **employer's** ground. A sister company that is not the employer cannot rely on it for its own purposes.
- **Needed structure [R]:**
  1. Each employing entity is the fiduciary for its employees.
  2. A shared HRMS run by one entity acts as **processor** for the others under an intra-group processing agreement (s.8(2)).
  3. A host entity receiving a secondee's data processes it under the employer's instructions, or, if it independently directs the work, on its own s.7(i) basis as a de facto employer. That needs legal review **[K]**.
  4. The privacy notice in each appointment letter names the group entities and the purposes: shared HRMS, payroll processing, secondment, group IT and e-mail provisioning.
- **Data separation.** Entity-scoped access (row-level security by `employing_entity_id`), cross-entity HR views only through explicit roles, and a log of every cross-entity access.

#### F5.2 Companies Act 2013: shared services between group companies
- **Related party (s.2(76))** [S: 43]:
  - Clause (iv) — "a private company in which a director or manager is a member or director".
  - Clause (viii) — holding, subsidiary or associate company, and a fellow subsidiary.
- **Private-company exemption (G.S.R. 464(E), 5 Jun 2015)** [S: 42, 43]:
  - For s.188 purposes, clause (viii) does not apply to a private company's holding, subsidiary, associate or fellow subsidiary.
  - From the notification of 13 Jun 2017, the exemption applies only to a company with no default in filing financial statements (s.137) or annual returns (s.92).
  - **Clause (iv) is not exempted.** Infrabyx sister companies with common directors are therefore usually still related parties for s.188. This is the likely position for Equator, Finbyx and Udofin, whose shareholding chain is not confirmed **[K: CS]**.
  - The second proviso to s.188(1) (interested members barred from voting) does not apply to private companies [S: 42].
  - The exemptions are not available to a private company that is a subsidiary of a public company [S: 42].
- **s.188 mechanics** (standard; text not re-opened **[K: CS to confirm proviso numbering and r.15(3) thresholds]**):
  - s.188(1)(d) — "availing or rendering of any services".
  - Transactions in the ordinary course of business **and** at arm's length are outside s.188.
  - Otherwise: Board approval, and an ordinary resolution where Companies (Meetings of Board and its Powers) Rules 2014 r.15(3) thresholds are crossed.
  - s.184 disclosure of interest; the s.189 register (MBP-4); AOC-2 disclosure through the Board's report (s.134(3)(h)); s.177(4)(iv) only if an Audit Committee is mandatory.
  - The pack (Appendix O §2.10, LR-CORP-001) already states this for the Institute referral. Extend it to shared services, cross-charges and the directory GDSA **[R]**.
- **Inter-company service agreement [R].** Contents:
  - scope and SLAs;
  - cost base and mark-up method (benchmarked);
  - invoicing and GST;
  - data protection (role matrix, processing schedule, breach cooperation, erasure);
  - confidentiality and ethical walls;
  - IP;
  - non-solicitation;
  - audit rights;
  - termination and transition.
- **GST [S: 40, 41]:**
  - Each company is a separate taxable person.
  - Cross-charges for shared staff or services are supplies between related persons (CGST Schedule I and valuation rules; not re-opened **[K]**).
  - For **secondment**, *CCE v Northern Operating Systems* (SC, 19 May 2022, CA 2289–2293/2021) used a substance test (control, payroll and lien, right to terminate, nature of skills) to find who the real employer was, and treated the arrangement as a supply of manpower.
  - CBIC Instruction 05/2023-GST (13 Dec 2023) says the judgment must not be applied mechanically; each arrangement turns on its contract.
  - Schedule III entry 1 excludes only services by an employee *to their employer*.
- **Transfer pricing.**
  - Domestic transfer pricing under the 1961 Act (s.92BA) applied only to narrow "specified domestic transactions". The Income-tax Act 2025 equivalent was not confirmed.
  - Nor was the successor to the s.40A(2) disallowance for excessive payments to related persons **[K: tax adviser]**.
  - Benchmark cross-charges at arm's length anyway, because s.188 needs it **[R]**.

#### F5.3 Labour law: deputation and secondment
- **Labour Codes.** The four Labour Codes came into force on **21 Nov 2025** (Code on Social Security: S.O. 5319(E); Code on Wages: S.O. 5322(E)) [S: 39].
  - The ESI Act 1948 was reported repealed from 21 Nov 2025.
  - The same report gives an EPF Act repeal date (3 May 2023) that looks inconsistent and was **not relied on [K]**.
  - Whether the Code rules are final was not confirmed **[K]**.
- **Who is the employer [R]:**
  - The appointing entity stays the employer, paying wages and making PF/ESI contributions under the Code on Social Security, unless employment is transferred.
  - A secondment letter should record:
    - that the lien stays with the employer;
    - who controls day-to-day work;
    - that the right to terminate stays with the employer;
    - the cost-recharge basis;
    - confidentiality to both entities;
    - return on expiry.
  - Without this, the host may be treated as the real employer (the *Northern Operating Systems* factors) or as a principal employer of contract labour. OSH Code thresholds and licensing apply to the latter **[K]**.
- **Cross-functional staff working for several group companies:**
  - either (i) one employer with documented secondments and recharges; or
  - (ii) separate part-time appointments with each entity, each paying and contributing on its share of wages. This option needs payroll and PF advice to avoid double-ceiling or under-contribution issues **[K]**.
- **Confidentiality and neutrality (the key risk) [R]:**
  - Staff who work for both Equator and Udofin or Finbyx (lender-side businesses) must never see Equator case data on matters involving those lenders' borrowers.
  - Use ethical walls, SoD rules (pack Appendix S §6), access logging and annual attestation. This applies NN-16 to people as well as systems.

## Design implications for Equator

All items are **[R]** unless a citation is given. "OFF" means a feature flag that defaults OFF and is gated by the named approver.

**A. Directory architecture**
1. **Build three separate surfaces, each with its own tables and no join path between them:**

   | Surface | What it holds | Paid placement |
   |---|---|---|
   | (i) Equator Neutral Directory `/neutrals` | Institutional neutrals | Never (D2-14 / NN-28 unchanged) |
   | (ii) Private Neutral Marketplace `/marketplace` | Chambers and independent non-advocate neutrals, booked by the party | Possible, rules A6–A10 |
   | (iii) Professional Panel Register | Advocates, CAs, CSs, CMAs; tenant-private inside each lender or enterprise tenant, shareable across group companies only under rule D | Never |

2. **Professional Panel Register defaults:**
   - Not public, no SEO, `noindex`.
   - Visible only to authenticated tenant users who engage professionals for their own matters (execution, registration, NPA suits).
   - The flag `directory.public_professionals` is **OFF** until counsel's written opinion and the Supreme Court decides SLP (C) 25794/2024.
3. **Advocate profile fields:** hard allow-list = the r.36 Schedule [S: 9; P: J2-090]:
   - name;
   - address, phone, e-mail;
   - enrolment number, date, original and current State Bar Council, Bar Association;
   - qualifications;
   - areas of practice.
   - Add Equator's verification badge text (Appendix S §4.5).
   - Languages and courts are filter metadata only, hidden, pending counsel (J2-090).
   - CI lint `R36_PROFILE_ALLOW_LIST`.
4. **Never shown for advocates, CAs, CSs or CMAs:**
   - ratings, reviews, testimonials;
   - fees or fee ranges;
   - client names or logos;
   - win or recovery statistics;
   - "featured", "top", "recommended" or "expert" labels;
   - photos or video (OFF pending OI-03);
   - "hire now" calls to action.
   - Display order: alphabetical or seeded-random only.
5. **CA, CS and CMA entries:** uniform, alphabetical, verification-only entries in the tenant register. Each regime is a separate flag, OFF until that institute's position is confirmed:
   - `register.ca` (ICAI 2019 aggregator bar [15]);
   - `register.cma` (ICMAI 2025 aggregator bar [17]);
   - `register.cs` (ICSI 2020 Guidelines [16]).

**B. Paid "preference" placement (new v3 revenue stream)**

6. **Scope:**
   - Allowed only on surface (ii), only for **non-advocate private-practice neutrals and chambers**.
   - For advocate-neutrals: `marketplace.paid_placement.advocates` = **OFF**, unlockable only by a written counsel opinion recorded in the legal-flag register.
   - Professionals on surface (iii): **never**.
7. **Presentation:**
   - A separate, visually distinct "Sponsored" slot above or beside organic results, labelled "Sponsored — paid placement. Does not affect Equator appointments."
   - Never interleaved with organic results.
   - At most N slots per page (configurable; default 2); rotation among sponsors.
   - Equal rate card for all eligible sponsors.
   - Meets E-Commerce r.4(12) [25] and Dark Patterns "disguised advertisement" [28].
8. **Organic ranking:**
   - Default seeded-random or alphabetical. Any other ranking parameter is published in plain language, in descending order of significance (E-Commerce r.5(3)(f) as amended [25]).
   - Ranking code is versioned. Ranking parameters are a governed (maker-checker) setting, not tenant-editable.
9. **Firewall:**
   - The existing `NO_PAID_PLACEMENT` CI test is extended to fail on any read path from `marketplace.sponsorships` or any billing table to `directory.neutral_public_view`, the allocation service, eligibility, Quality Index, grading or conflict checks.
   - Sponsorship status is never visible to case managers, registrars or the Panel Bench.
   - In an Equator-administered case, the party-facing neutral views never show sponsored slots.
10. **Disclosure:**
    - Where a party in any matter (administered or private) chooses a sponsoring neutral, the neutral's s.12(1) disclosure template includes "I pay Equator for marketplace visibility".
    - Counterparties see Equator's group relationships with any party (NN-16 extended to Infrabyx group clients of Finbyx/Udofin).
11. **Governance:**
    - D2-14 changes only through a Board decision-log entry: "D2-14 retained for the Equator Neutral Directory; paid placement permitted on the Private Marketplace subject to rules 6–10".
    - The flag `marketplace.paid_placement` is **OFF** until Board approval and counsel sign-off.
    - The annual dark-pattern self-audit certificate (r.4(15)) covers this surface.

**C. Fees, referrals and party choice**

12. **No fee-share with advocates:** no commission on, or percentage of, an advocate's fee, and no listing fee charged to advocates (Ch. III r.2; r.36). Tariff lint blocks the tariff tags `advocate_commission` and `advocate_listing_fee`.
13. **No referral rewards of any kind (cash or credits) to advocates** until counsel clears; this supersedes Appendix O X-11's "non-cash credits" (J-107). The same applies to CAs and CMAs (ICMAI referral-network bar).
14. **Party-side fees:**
    - Only for Equator's own services, priced the same whatever professional is engaged.
    - Never labelled as a fee for "finding a lawyer".
    - The "Find an advocate" signposting stays free (Appendix O X-01).
15. **Marketplace take** (O-R2) applies only to non-advocate neutrals and chambers bookings. It is Equator's own GST-able supply.
16. **Party choice:**
    - The party always sees the organic list.
    - Sponsored slots are dismissible and never pre-selected.
    - No nudges ("most booked", countdowns).
    - Booking requires an explicit confirmation step (r.4(9)).

**D. Data model, consent and cross-group sharing**

17. **`directory.professional_records` fields:**
    - `professional_type`;
    - `display_fields[]` (allow-list by type);
    - `consent_id` per purpose, with a separate `consent_group_share` naming the recipient entities;
    - `source` (`self_entered` | `staff_verified_official_portal` | `council_api_permitted` | `institute_certificate`);
    - `source_url`, `source_captured_at`, `evidence_hash`;
    - `verified_by[2]`, `verified_at`, `reverify_due` (CoP 5-year cycle + annual Equator check);
    - `status` (`active` | `lapsed` | `suspended_by_council` | `withdrawn`);
    - `last_confirmed_by_professional_at`;
    - `erasure_requested_at`.
18. **Freshness rules:**
    - Records not reconfirmed in 12 months show "details last confirmed {date}".
    - At 18 months they are hidden from search until reconfirmed (s.8(3)).
19. **No bulk import:**
    - No bulk import or scraping of Bar Council rolls or institute directories.
    - `KYC_PORT.lookupBarRoll.{state}` stays OFF until that Council's written permission is on file [P].
    - CI lint blocks scheduled jobs that call roll-portal hosts.
20. **No reliance on s.3(c)(ii):** treat every record as consent-based. Show the DPDP notice at profile creation, with per-field display consent and a withdrawal path equal in ease to sign-up.
21. **Group Data-Sharing Agreement (GDSA)** between the Directory Owner, Finbyx, Udofin and each consuming entity, covering:
    - a purpose schedule (execution, registration, recovery engagement only; no marketing, no credit scoring of professionals);
    - the role matrix (independent fiduciaries vs processor);
    - data minimisation (allow-list fields + verification status only);
    - an onward-transfer ban;
    - an erasure and correction propagation SLA (≤ 72 h);
    - breach cooperation;
    - audit rights;
    - termination and deletion.
    - Approved under s.188 where it involves consideration.
22. **Event-driven propagation:** `directory.record.updated|withdrawn|erased.v1` events reach every consuming tenant; consumers must apply them. A reconciliation job reports drift.
23. **Hard separation:** neutral, allocation and case data are never shared with Finbyx/Udofin, and lender-side panel performance notes never enter Equator's neutral systems (NN-16). CI test `GROUP_SHARE_SCOPE`.

**E. Group operations and HR**

24. **Employer of record:** each employee has one `employing_entity_id`. Secondments are recorded as `hr.secondments` with:

    - dates;
    - control and termination clauses;

25. **Shared HRMS:** row-level security by entity. Cross-entity HR roles are elevated, maker-checker, and logged. An intra-group processing agreement covers it (s.8(2)), and the employee notice names all group entities and purposes.
26. **Ethical walls:**
    - Users with roles in Finbyx/Udofin get no Equator case-data roles for matters involving those companies' clients.
    - The SoD check runs at role grant.
    - Annual attestation.
27. **Shared-services register:**
    - every inter-company agreement, its arm's-length basis, the s.188 approval reference and the AOC-2 flag;
    - GST cross-charge invoices generated from it;
    - a tax-adviser review flag for secondment GST (*Northern Operating Systems*).
