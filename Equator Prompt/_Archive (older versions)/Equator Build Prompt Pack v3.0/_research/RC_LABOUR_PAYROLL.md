### F2. Code on Wages 2019 [1]

**F2.1 "Employee" and "wages".** s.2(k): "employee" covers skilled, unskilled, manual, supervisory, managerial, administrative, technical and clerical work (apprentices and Armed Forces excluded) — so CoW protections (minimum wage, timely payment, deductions, equal pay) apply to **all** Equator and chamber staff, including managers [1]. s.2(y): wages = basic pay + dearness allowance + retaining allowance; excluded items are (a) bonus outside the terms of employment, (b) value of house accommodation/amenities excluded by order, (c) employer PF/pension contribution and interest, (d) conveyance allowance/travel concession, (e) special-expense reimbursement, (f) HRA, (g) award/settlement amounts, (h) overtime allowance, (i) commission, (j) gratuity, (k) retrenchment compensation/retirement benefit/ex gratia on termination [1].
- **50% rule (first proviso):** if items (a)–(i) exceed one-half (or a notified percentage) of total remuneration, the excess is deemed wages [1].
- **Second proviso:** for equal-pay and wage-payment purposes, items (d), (f), (g) and (h) count as wages [1].
- **In-kind cap:** remuneration in kind counts as wages up to 15% of total wages [1][3].
- **MoLE interpretation** [4][5]: one definition across all four Codes (Jan FAQ Q6); worked example — total ₹76,000, cap ₹38,000, allowances ₹40,000, excess ₹2,000 added to basic+DA ₹20,000 → statutory wages ₹22,000 (Q7); gratuity and retrenchment compensation are outside the 50% test (Q3); leave encashment is not an "allowance" (Q5); performance incentives, ESOPs, variable pay and reimbursements are not wages (Q3); annual performance incentives are not wages (Mar FAQ Q4); overtime allowance *is* counted in the 50% test (Mar FAQ Q1a, Q8); employer PF and statutory bonus are excluded from the illustration base, which uses gross salary excluding statutory contributions (Mar FAQ Q1, Q1b); food coupons, ration items and mobile recharge are remuneration in kind (Mar FAQ Q18); the revised definition operates from 21 Nov 2025 and gratuity applies it prospectively (Mar FAQ Q6, Q7, Q11).
- **Change management:** a restructure to comply may need IR Code s.40 notice of change for "workers", and CoSS s.124 prohibits reducing wages or benefits to avoid contributions [V-sec 10; s.40 notice period of 21 days is K, not re-fetched].

**F2.2 Minimum wage and floor wage.** s.5 (no payment below notified minimum); s.6 (fixed by time or piece; hourly/daily/monthly; skill levels unskilled/semi-skilled/skilled/highly skilled; geographical area; optional arduous-conditions factor); s.8(4) review at intervals not exceeding 5 years; s.9 Central floor wage, which State minimums may not undercut [1]. Wages (Central) Rules r.3: fixed on a day basis; hourly = daily ÷ 8; monthly = daily × 26; r.4 VDA revised before 1 April and 1 October each year on CPI-IW; r.10 floor wage revised at ≤ 5-year intervals [V-sec 6]. **Floor wage figures are not yet notified** [V-sec 7].

**F2.3 Wage period and payment timelines.** s.16 wage period not to exceed one month; s.17(1) daily — end of shift; weekly — last working day before weekly holiday; fortnightly — before end of the second day after the fortnight; **monthly — before the 7th day of the following month**; s.17(2) **within 2 working days** of removal, dismissal, retrenchment, resignation or closure-related unemployment [1][3]. (Pack LR-LAB-002, LR-LAB-016.)

**F2.4 Mode of payment.** s.15: coin, currency notes, cheque, credit to the employee's bank account, or electronic mode; the appropriate Government may require specified establishments to pay only by cheque or bank credit [1]. (Older State S&E text, e.g. Delhi S&E Act s.19(4) "cash" [26], is superseded on wage-payment matters by the CoW — **[K/counsel confirm]**.)

**F2.5 Deductions.** s.18: only listed deductions (fines, absence, damage/loss, housing/amenities, advances and loans, statutory levies and court orders, social-security contributions, co-operative dues, union fees with consent, etc.); **total ≤ 50% of wages per wage period** (s.18(3)); excess only as prescribed (s.18(4)) [1]. Central Rules r.13: excess carried forward and recovered in later periods, still capped at 50% of each month; r.16 fines need 7 days' show-cause and imposition/intimation within 15 days; r.17 absence deductions need prior intimation and 7-day reply; r.15 notice of acts/omissions in Hindi, English and local language; damage/loss deductions need prior intimation to the employee only (final rules) [V-sec 6, 7].

**F2.6 Overtime and hours (CoW side).** s.13 normal working day and weekly rest as prescribed; s.14 overtime at **not less than twice** the normal rate [1]. Central Rules r.5: 8-hour normal working day for daily wage periods; weekly hours ≤ 48 for other periods; r.6 weekly rest day, substitution allowed but never more than 10 consecutive days without rest; rest-day work at overtime rates [V-sec 6]. MoLE: overtime entitlement attaches to employees whose minimum rate is fixed under the Code (Mar FAQ Q5); overtime due at end of wage period (Q24) [5].

**F2.7 Bonus.** Chapter IV applies to establishments employing **20 or more** persons on any day in the accounting year (s.41(2)) [1]; eligibility — wages not exceeding a **notified** monthly amount and ≥ 30 days' work in the year; minimum 8.33% of wages or ₹100 (higher), maximum 20% (s.26) [1][3]. **The eligibility and calculation ceilings under s.26 are not confirmed** (MoLE FAQs are silent [4][5]; pack LR-LAB-018 "ceilings UNVERIFIED"). The repealed Payment of Bonus Act values (₹21,000 eligibility; ₹7,000 or minimum wage calculation ceiling) are **[K, not confirmed as saved]**. Central Rules rr.21–28 and Appendices A–D govern set-on/set-off and allocable surplus computation; r.21 principal pays minimum bonus where a contractor defaults [V-sec 6].

**F2.8 Equal remuneration.** s.3: no gender discrimination in wages for same or similar work, in recruitment or in conditions of employment; no wage reduction to achieve compliance; s.4 authority decides "same or similar work" [1]. Pay-equity analytics in the HRMS should compute on the s.2(y) second-proviso basis (including HRA, conveyance, overtime).

**F2.9 Registers, wage slips and records.** s.50: registers of employees, muster roll and wages; notice display; wage slips (s.50(4) exemption only for ≤ 5 agricultural/domestic workers) [1]. Central Rules r.51: Form I (Employee Register), Form IV (Wages, Overtime, Advances, Fines and Deductions), Form IX (Attendance-cum-Muster Roll), electronic or physical, **retained 5 years after the last entry**; r.52 wage slip Form V on or before payment; r.45/Form VII nomination; undisbursed dues deposited after 3/6 months, unclaimed held 7 years (rr.45–47) [V-sec 6]. MoLE handbook: notice board (minimum wages, hours, wage period, pay date, Inspector-cum-Facilitator) in English, Hindi and local language [3].

**F2.10 Claims.** s.45 claims authority; claim within 3 years (s.45(6)); appeal 90 days (s.49); r.50 employer appeal admitted only on deposit; composition at 50% of maximum fine (r.54) [V-sec 6; pack LR-LAB-013].

### F3. Code on Social Security 2020

**F3.1 EPF / EPS / EDLI.**
- **Applicability:** establishments with **20 or more** employees [3]; voluntary coverage below that is possible under the Schemes **[K]**.
- **Rates:** the MoLE handbook summarises ss.15–16 as employer 10% of wages with an equal employee share [3]; the EPF Scheme 2026 regime applied in practice is **12% employee + 12% employer**, the employer share split **8.33% EPS (on wages up to the ceiling) + 3.67% EPF**, plus **EDLI 0.50%** and **administrative charges 0.50%** [V-sec 10, 11]. (The 10% rate applies only to classes notified for it — **[K]**; keep as a parameter.)
- **Wage ceiling:** **₹25,000 per month from 17 Sep 2026** (S.O. 5109(E), issued under CoSS s.2(89)), replacing ₹15,000 (last revised 1 Sep 2014; retained by S.O. 2702(E), 29 May 2026) [V-sec 9, 10, 11]. Maximum EPS contribution rises from ₹1,250 to ≈ ₹2,083 per month [9][11]. Employees earning ₹15,001–₹25,000 become mandatorily covered [9]. The ceiling applies to statutory "wages" (s.2(88)), not gross [9].
- **September 2026 split month:** ₹15,000 for 1–16 Sep and ₹25,000 for 17–30 Sep, pro rata (secondary interpretation; EPFO guidance awaited) [9][11]. September ECR due 15 Oct 2026 [11].
- **Voluntary higher wages:** employers may contribute on a higher wage base and employees may contribute voluntarily on a higher base [V-sec 10]. EPS coverage: employees with wages up to ₹25,000 who are EPF members must be in EPS; those previously outside EPS because of the old ceiling generally remain outside [V-sec 10].
- **International Workers:** ceiling does not apply; 12% of entire wages without cap (clarified by the corrigenda); IW regime under challenge in *LG Electronics India Ltd v Union of India* (SC, pending) [V-sec 10].
- **Appeal:** EPF assessment appeal to the Industrial Tribunal needs a 25% deposit (CoSS s.23) [pack LR-LAB-020].

**F3.2 ESI.**
- **Applicability:** establishments with **10 or more** persons (except seasonal factories); one employee in hazardous work [3].
- **Coverage wage threshold:** **₹21,000 per month** continues until rules are finalised; the CoSS wage definition applies from 21 Nov 2025 (MoLE Mar FAQ Q12; Jan FAQ Q17) [4][5]; unchanged after the EPF revision [11].
- **Rates:** **employee 0.75%, employer 3.25%** [V-sec 11]. Employer deducts and pays both shares (s.31) [3].
- Contribution periods/benefit periods (Apr–Sep / Oct–Mar) and the rule that an employee crossing the threshold mid-period stays covered to the period's end are **[K, not re-fetched]**.

**F3.3 Gratuity (Chapter V, ss.53–58).** Applies to shops/establishments with **10 or more** employees [3]. 15 days' wages per completed year (or part over six months) on last drawn wages; 5 years' continuous service, not required on death, disablement or expiry of fixed-term employment; **fixed-term employees eligible after 1 year** of service under the contract (an 11-month engagement does not qualify); maximum **₹20 lakh**; payable within 30 days (s.56); nomination after 1 year (s.55) [3][4][5]. Contractor is the employer of contract labour for gratuity (Mar FAQ Q16) [5]. Gratuity on the new wage definition applies to exits on/after 21 Nov 2025 (Mar FAQ Q17; Jan FAQ Q8–Q9) [4][5]. Monthly-rated divisor 26 [pack LR-LAB-009]. The ₹20 lakh figure is the MoLE FAQ statement; the pack notes no fresh CoSS s.53(3) notification was found (old PGA S.O. 1420(E), 2018, saved) [45].

**F3.4 Maternity benefit (Chapter VI, ss.59–72).** Eligibility 80 days' work in the preceding 12 months; **26 weeks**; medical bonus **₹3,500**; miscarriage/MTP 6 weeks; tubectomy 2 weeks; two nursing breaks until the child is 15 months; dismissal during maternity absence prohibited; crèche at **50 or more** employees [3]. CoSS Central Rules: crèche (CCTV, trained staff) **or crèche allowance ≥ ₹500 per child per month** [V-sec 7]. Work-from-home after maternity leave by mutual agreement (old MB Act s.5(5) equivalent) — **[K; CoSS section number not confirmed]**.

**F3.5 Employees' compensation (Chapter VII, ss.73–91).** Replaces the EC Act 1923; claim within 2 years, condonable (s.82); settlement memorandum registered with the competent authority (s.89) [pack 44, 45]. ESI-covered employees claim through ESI. For remote staff, "arising out of and in the course of employment" at a home workplace is unsettled — India Briefing flags the ambiguity [39].

**F3.6 Gig and platform workers (Chapter IX).** Definitions s.2(35) (gig worker), s.2(60)–(61) (platform work/worker); registration s.113; schemes s.114 (aggregator contribution 1–2% of turnover capped at 5% of payouts — s.114(5) not commenced) [3][44]. CoSS Central Rules: aggregators register workers on the designated portal **within 45 days** [V-sec 7]; contributions credited to the Central Social Security Fund once notified (Mar FAQ Q15) [5].

**F3.7 Registration and returns.** EPF 20, ESI 10, gratuity 10 (above). OSH registration (s.3) for 10+ workers, online, within 60 days; changes within 30 days; single electronic annual return replacing 31 earlier returns [3]. OSH Central Rules: Form I on the Shram Suvidha portal; certificate (Form III) within 7 days or deemed; late fee after 60 days; existing establishments update within 6 months [V-sec 8].

### F4. OSH Code 2020 [2]

**F4.1 Who is a "worker".** s.2(1)(zzl): any person employed to do manual, unskilled, skilled, **technical, operational, clerical** or supervisory work, excluding persons employed **mainly in a managerial or administrative capacity** and supervisors drawing wages above ₹18,000/month (or notified amount) [2]; MoLE Jan FAQ Q14 confirms the ₹18,000 supervisory exclusion [4]. **Product consequence:** Equator case managers, developers, support agents, paralegals and clerks are "workers" regardless of salary unless their work is mainly managerial/administrative; team leads who mainly supervise and earn above ₹18,000 are not. Classification must be recorded per employee with reasons and reviewed annually.

**F4.2 Applicability thresholds.** "Establishment" = place with **10 or more workers** (s.2(1)(v)); "factory" = 20 workers with power / 40 without (s.2(1)(w)) [2]. Contract labour chapter applies at **50 or more** contract workers; licence valid 5 years (s.48); principal employer liable if contractor short-pays (s.55) [3]. Crèche at more than 50 workers (s.24 per handbook) [3].

**F4.3 Appointment letter.** s.6(1)(f): employer must issue a **letter of appointment to every employee** in the prescribed form at appointment; existing employees without one must receive it **within three months** of commencement [2]. Prescribed format provided in OSH Central Rules [V-sec 7].

**F4.4 Hours, rest and overtime.** s.25(1): no worker to work more than **8 hours a day**, with intervals and spread-over as notified [2]; OSH Central Rules: **48 hours per week** [V-sec 8]; s.26 no more than 6 days in a week, compensatory holidays [2]; s.27 overtime at **twice** the normal rate, computed daily or weekly whichever is more favourable, **with the worker's consent** [2]; Central Rules: overtime beyond 8 h/day (daily-wage) or 48 h/week (others); rounding 15–30 min = 30 min, > 30 min = 1 hour; daily rate = monthly ÷ 26 [V-sec 8]. Quarterly overtime cap under the OSH Central Rules — **not confirmed**. MoLE Mar FAQ Q24: 8 h/day, 48 h/week, overtime at twice, paid at end of wage period [5].

**F4.5 Annual leave with wages — s.32.** [2]
- (i) eligibility: worked **180 days or more** in the calendar year;
- (ii) accrual: **1 day for every 20 days** worked (adolescents and below-ground mine workers 1 per 15);
- (iii) lay-off, maternity leave and annual leave count toward the 180 days but do not earn leave; (iv) holidays within leave are excluded; (v) a worker joining after 1 January earns leave if he has worked one-fourth of the remaining days of the year;
- (vi) on discharge, dismissal, quitting, superannuation or death, wages in lieu of accrued leave are paid — **within 2 working days** (discharge, dismissal, quitting) or **within 2 months** (superannuation, death);
- (vii) carry-forward capped at **30 days**; leave applied for and refused may be carried forward without limit;
- (viii) encashment on demand at year-end; (ix) leave above 30 days is encashable;
- s.32(2) Government may extend the section to other establishments; s.32(3) more favourable award/agreement/contract terms prevail (mines) [2].
- MoLE Mar FAQ [5]: applies to workers and supervisors earning ≤ ₹18,000 (Q20), sales-promotion employees included (Q22); no cap on encashment; leave above 30 days refused can be encashed at year-end; leave to credit encashable on separation (Q26); **the OSH Code prevails over inconsistent State law, but the employee gets any more favourable State benefit** (Q25).

**F4.6 Women and night work.** s.43: women may work in all establishments; before 6:00 a.m. or after 7:00 p.m. only with consent and prescribed safety measures [3]. OSH Central Rules conditions: written consent; no conflict with maternity provisions; safe pick-up and drop; well-lit workplace with toilets, drinking water and CCTV; emergency numbers displayed at work and in vehicles; POSH compliance [V-sec 8]. For remote night work from home, transport conditions are inapplicable in fact, but consent, POSH and safety-helpline duties still apply **[recommendation; counsel confirm]**.

**F4.7 Registers and records.** s.33: registers electronically or otherwise (work, hours, rest days, wages, leave, overtime, attendance, dangerous occurrences); notices; wage slips; returns to the Inspector-cum-Facilitator [2][3]. Annual free health check-ups are an OSH duty for notified classes; Central Rules specify 40+ age for dock and construction work [3][8] — not applicable to office staff unless notified.

### F5. Industrial Relations Code 2020

- **"Worker"** s.2(zr) mirrors the OSH exclusions (managerial/administrative; supervisory above ₹18,000) [pack 44].
- **Standing orders:** mandatory for industrial establishments with **300 or more workers** (s.28) [3]; Model Standing Orders 2026 deemed adopted until certified orders take effect (s.29(2)); adoption intimated electronically and recorded under IR (Central) Rules r.47 [V-sec 12]. ### F6. State Shops & Establishments Acts (launch states DL, UP, RJ, MH, KA)

**F6.1 Interplay with the OSH Code.** State S&E Acts have not been repealed by the Codes. MoLE's position is that the OSH Code prevails over inconsistent State law while the employee keeps any more favourable State benefit (Mar FAQ Q25) [5]. Karnataka has gone further: by Karnataka Act No. 38 of 2026 (Gazette 4 Sep 2026, immediate effect) establishments employing **10 or more workers already registered under the OSH Code are excluded** from the Karnataka S&CE Act 1961 [V-sec 32, 33]. Other States are moving their S&E Acts toward the Code's deregulatory model (higher thresholds, longer daily hours, 144-hour quarterly overtime caps). The rule engine must therefore evaluate, per employee and per day, the **union** of applicable floors (Code + State S&E + contract) and apply the most favourable per entitlement.

**F6.3 Which State law applies to a remote employee.** No primary source opened settles this; India Briefing describes it only as "compliance fragmentation" [39]. Analysis **[recommendation; counsel to confirm]**: (a) S&E Acts regulate *establishments* located in the State; a home is not an "establishment" of the employer unless the employer carries on business there. A remote employee is therefore ordinarily attached to the establishment that employs, controls and pays him (e.g. Equator's Delhi or Noida office) for S&E purposes. (b) Labour Codes apply irrespective of location, and the "appropriate Government" follows the establishment. (c) **Professional tax and Labour Welfare Fund** are levied by the State where the person is "employed"/works; practice for permanent remote staff varies and some employers register in the State of residence — treat as a CA opinion item. (d) Minimum wage: apply the higher of the establishment's State and the work-location State as a protective default. (e) POSH: the "workplace" includes places visited and, for remote work, the virtual workplace [K; India Briefing notes POSH covers virtual workplaces 39]. The HRMS must therefore store both `establishment_state` and `work_location_state` per employee per effective date.

### F9. POSH obligations (summary; detail in pack LR-POSH-001…011)

Every employer with **10 or more employees** constitutes an Internal Committee; below 10 (and complaints against the employer) go to the Local Committee (POSH Act ss.4, 6). IC: senior woman Presiding Officer, ≥ 2 employee members, 1 external member, at least half women; external member fee ≥ ₹200/day (r.3). Complaint within 3 + 3 months; inquiry 90 days; annual report to employer and District Officer; complaint numbers in the Board's report (Companies (Accounts) Rules r.8(5)(x)) [45]. For a remote-first company the IC must be reachable virtually, and the "workplace" includes the virtual workplace [39]. Chambers with fewer than 10 staff must display Local Committee details and still have a policy [recommendation].

### F12. Workforce monitoring — legality

**F12.1 Legal frame.**
- **DPDP Act 2023.** Core obligations (ss.3–17 incl. s.5 notice, s.6 consent, s.7 legitimate uses, s.8 duties) commence **13 May 2027**; build-to-comply now (pack LR-DPDP-001; NN-15) [45]. **s.7(i)** permits processing without consent "for the purposes of employment or those related to safeguarding the employer from loss or liability", illustrated by prevention of corporate espionage, confidentiality of trade secrets, IP and classified information, and provision of a service or benefit sought by an employee [V-sec paraphrase 40, 41; enacted wording to be quoted from the Gazette text in the spec]. Two firm/academic views [40][41] agree s.7(i) is **not a blanket licence**: purpose limitation and the illustrative, security-focused examples narrow it; KS&K treats attendance and login records, corporate email for security/business purposes, application/website logs and CCTV (excluding washrooms/changing rooms) as easier to justify, and screen capture/recording, **keystroke logging**, BYOD monitoring and off-hours location tracking as high-risk; personal email and private messages should be excluded [41]. LiveLaw argues that a monitoring "consent" clause signed with a job offer is not free consent (s.6; Contract Act s.14) and that a clause defeating the DPDP Act is void under Contract Act s.23 [40] (opinion, not settled law). Security safeguards and ≥ 1-year log retention (s.8(5); Rule 6), breach reporting within 72 hours (Rule 7), erasure when purpose served (s.8(7); Rule 8) [45].
- **IT Act 2000 / SPDI Rules 2011.** s.43A (body-corporate compensation for negligent handling of sensitive personal data) remains until DPDP s.44(2) omits it on 13 May 2027 [45]; biometric attendance data is "sensitive personal data" under the SPDI Rules **[K]**; s.66E (capturing images of a private area) and s.72A (disclosure in breach of lawful contract) are criminal limits on webcam/home capture and on sharing monitoring data **[K]**.
- **Constitutional privacy.** *K.S. Puttaswamy v Union of India* (2017) 10 SCC 1 recognises informational privacy and a proportionality standard (legality, legitimate aim, necessity/least-restrictive means, procedural safeguards) [41; elements K]. It binds the State directly; KS&K notes the DPDP Act does not expressly import the test for private employers but recommends applying it [41]; LiveLaw relies on *Kaushal Kishor* for horizontal effect of Arts. 19/21 [40] (contested).
- **Labour law hooks.** Model Standing Orders allow attendance by ID card, biometrics or notified methods and make unauthorised IT access misconduct [12]; habitual late attendance may lead to CoW-compliant deductions [12]; OSH s.33 requires attendance and hours registers [2] — so *some* time recording is legally required for "workers", which is the strongest legitimate-use anchor.

**F12.2 Acceptable vs. high-risk practices (synthesis; recommendation).**

| Practice | Position | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Login/logoff to company systems, shift check-in, timesheets | Acceptable — needed for OSH s.33 registers, overtime and CoW deductions | Notice; employee can view and dispute; no inference beyond attendance |

### F13. Whistle-blower / vigil mechanism and conflict-of-interest declarations

- **Companies Act 2013 s.177(9)–(10)** with **Companies (Meetings of Board and its Powers) Rules 2014 r.7**: a vigil mechanism is mandatory for every **listed** company, companies that **accept deposits from the public**, and companies that have **borrowed more than ₹50 crore from banks and public financial institutions**; s.177(10) requires safeguards against victimisation and direct access to the audit committee chairperson (or a director nominated by the Board where there is no audit committee) [K; pack V5 §3.2]. Equator, a private company, is **not** required unless it crosses the borrowing or deposit limb — a voluntary mechanism is good practice and is already part of the pack's whistle-blower desk (P09) [44]. Group companies in the Infrabyx ecosystem that are NBFCs or listed (e.g. a listed NBFC client) are subject to their own regimes (LODR Reg. 22 for listed entities) [K].
- **Conflict of interest.** Directors disclose interests under Companies Act s.184 (MBP-1) and related-party transactions are governed by s.188 [K]. Employees have no general statutory COI form; MSO treats harmful moonlighting without permission as misconduct [12]. For an ODR institution, staff COI is also an independence issue (A&C Act s.12 disclosures for neutrals; institutional staff must not have undisclosed links to parties) [K; pack App. N]. Multi-company group staff need declarations per group entity.

### F14. Private ADR Chambers' staff (small establishments)

- **Always applicable regardless of size:** CoW (minimum wage, s.17 timelines, s.18 deductions, s.3 equal pay, s.50 records and wage slips) [1]; OSH s.6(1)(f) appointment letter is an employer duty under the Code, but the OSH Code's establishment definition starts at 10 workers [2] — **whether the appointment-letter duty binds an employer below 10 workers is NC**; issuing one is recommended in all cases.
- **Threshold-gated:** ESI 10; gratuity 10; POSH IC 10 (below → Local Committee); OSH establishment/registration and s.32 leave 10; EPF 20 (voluntary below); bonus 20; GRC 20; S&E registration — UP 20 [35], Maharashtra 20 (intimation below) [30], Delhi all (subject to the abeyance conflict) [26], Rajasthan possibly exempt 0–10 (NC), Karnataka per amended Act [33]. UP's 2025 amendment expressly brings medical practitioners and architects' offices within "commercial establishment" [35]; whether advocates' chambers are covered in each State is **NC**.
- **Contract staff vs employees.** Indicators of employment are control and supervision of how work is done, integration into the business, exclusivity, fixed hours and employer-provided tools (control/integration tests, e.g. *Dharangadhara Chemical Works v State of Saurashtra* AIR 1957 SC 264) [K]. Misclassified "consultants" carry CoW, CoSS and TDS exposure. Fixed-term employment under IR Code s.2(o) with pro rata benefits is the lawful alternative to rolling consultancies [44].
- **Advocates.** Bar Council of India Rules (Part VI, Ch. II, r.49) restrict a practising advocate from being a full-time salaried employee **[K]**; junior advocates in chambers are normally engaged on retainer/stipend as professionals, not as employees — the chamber module should model them as `professional_engagement`, not `employee`.
- **TDS on professional fees.** Payments to resident professionals (consultants, junior advocates, neutrals) — ITA 2025 **s.393(1) Table Sl. 6(iii)** (old s.194J), **10%** (2% for fees for technical services), threshold **₹50,000 per tax year** [pack LR-TAX-004, 45]. Contractor payments (old s.194C) fall under s.393 [17]; serial number, rate and threshold **NC**. Quarterly statement Form 140; certificate Form 131 [17].
- **Gig/platform angle.** If Equator's Marketplace or chamber tools let neutrals or case-support freelancers obtain paid work through the platform, Equator may arguably be an "aggregator" and those individuals "platform workers" under CoSS s.2 — triggering registration duties now (45-day portal registration under the Central Rules [7]) and contribution once s.114(5) commences [44]. **Open legal question; counsel opinion before Marketplace pay-outs go live.**

## Design implications for Equator

### A. Payroll rule-pack architecture

1. **Rule packs as data, not code.** Create `payroll.rule_params` with columns: `param_code`, `jurisdiction` (`IN`, `IN-DL`, `IN-UP`, `IN-RJ`, `IN-MH`, `IN-KA`, `IN-WB`, `IN-TN`, `IN-GJ`, …), `sphere` (`central`/`state`), `applies_to` (employee class: `worker`, `non_worker`, `fte`, `international_worker`, `apprentice`, `all`), `value` (JSON: scalar, slab table or formula reference), `unit`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `source_instrument` (e.g. "S.O. 5109(E), 17 Sep 2026"), `source_url`, `source_type` (`gazette`, `official_guidance`, `secondary`), `verification_status` (`VERIFIED`, `SECONDARY_ONLY`, `NOT_CONFIRMED`), `verified_by`, `verified_on`, `approved_by`, `approved_on`. ### B. Statutory components (salary structure engine)

6. **Component register** with a fixed `statutory_class` per component: `WAGE_CORE` (basic, DA, retaining allowance); `EXCLUDED_A_TO_I` (each tagged with its s.2(y) letter: HRA = f, conveyance = d, overtime = h, commission = i, contractual-bonus-outside-terms = a, employer PF = c, reimbursements = e …); `EXCLUDED_J_K` (gratuity, retrenchment, retirement, ex gratia); `NOT_WAGES` (performance incentives, ESOPs, variable pay, reimbursements [4]); `IN_KIND` (food coupons, mobile recharge [5]). Tenants may add components only by choosing a class; the class is locked once used in a finalised run.
7. **Statutory wage calculator** (`wage_code_base = core + max(0, excluded_a_i − cap% × total_remuneration)`) runs on every structure save and every pay run; it is the base for EPF, ESI, gratuity, bonus, leave encashment and overtime. Show the MoLE worked example (₹76,000 → ₹22,000) as a golden test [4]. Raise `wage_definition_warning` (pack LR-LAB-003) and block finalisation of a CTC template that yields core < 50% unless the add-back is applied.
8. **Earnings and deductions list (India default):** Basic, DA, HRA, conveyance, special allowance, overtime (2×), arrears, leave encashment, statutory bonus, gratuity, notice pay, reimbursements, in-kind perquisites; **deductions:** employee EPF (incl. VPF), employee ESI, PT, LWF (employee), TDS s.392, salary advance/loan EMI, approved fines (with show-cause evidence), absence, damage/loss (with prior intimation), court attachments, co-operative/union dues with consent. Employer contributions computed but shown on CTC statement: EPF, EPS, EDLI, admin, ESI, LWF, gratuity provision, NPS (≤ 14% deductible), bonus provision.
9. **50% deduction guard (HB):** total deductions per period ≤ 50% of wages; excess automatically deferred to the next period with an employee-visible schedule (r.13 [6]). Fines and damage deductions require attached evidence objects (notice, reply window expiry) before they can be posted.
10. **Pay calendar locks:** monthly run must be paid before the 7th of the following month (CoW s.17(1)); F&F clock `wrk_wages_2wd` starts at the separation date and the F&F run includes OSH s.32(1)(vi) leave encashment within 2 working days [1][2]; gratuity due within 30 days. Breach of any clock raises a red alert to HR head and CS.
11. **Outputs:** Form V-style wage slip on or before payment [6]; Form I / IV / IX registers generated electronically [6]; EPF ECR, ESI return, PT returns per State, LWF returns; **Form 130** annual certificate, **Form 123** perquisite statement, **Form 138** quarterly statement, intake of **Form 124** and **Form 127** [14][15][16]; CoW nomination Form VII. Form numbers for State returns are configurable.
12. **Tax engine:** regime choice per employee per tax year (default regime unless opt-out), monthly re-estimation, rebate s.156 with marginal relief [13], standard deduction, employer NPS. Never give tax advice in UI copy; show "computed under the Income-tax Act 2025 and Income-tax Rules 2026 as configured" with the parameter version.

### C. Leave and time policy — statutory floor vs company policy

13. **Two-layer leave model.** Layer 1 `statutory_floor` is computed by the engine per employee from (a) OSH s.32 if the employee is a `worker` in a 10+ establishment, (b) the State S&E Act of the `establishment_state` if covered, (c) any more favourable State benefit [5 Q25]. Layer 2 `company_policy` is tenant-configurable but may only be **equal to or more generous than** Layer 1 on every dimension (days, accrual, carry-forward, encashment). The validator rejects a policy that undercuts the floor for any employee class.
14. **Equator default policy (recommended for all staff, workers and managers alike, to avoid per-employee divergence):** earned leave 18 days/year accruing 1.5 days/month (≥ OSH 1:20 and MH/KA 18), eligibility from day one with pro-rating (more generous than the 180-day rule), carry-forward up to 45 days (meets Delhi/MH 45) with automatic encashment of the excess above 45 at calendar year-end (and above 30 for OSH workers who request it); casual leave 8 days; sick leave 12 days; 10 paid holidays (3 national mandatory + State festival list); refused leave never lapses; leave encashment on separation within 2 working days. Tenants (chambers) can choose "statutory minimum" or "Equator standard" templates.
15. **Holiday calendars** per State and year as data; national holidays locked (cannot be removed); festival holidays editable within the State-mandated count; remote employees get the calendar of their `establishment_state` plus an optional `work_location_state` festival swap.
16. **Hours and overtime:** default workday 8 h, week 40–48 h; any time above 8 h/day or 48 h/week for a `worker` is flagged as overtime requiring prior **consent** (OSH s.27) and paid at 2×; quarterly overtime cap per State parameter (144 h where amended); managerial staff tracked for wellbeing only. Weekly rest: never more than 10 consecutive days without rest [6].
17. **Women night work:** employees opting into work before 06:00 or after 19:00 record written consent (revocable) and see the safety helpline; office-based night work requires the transport/CCTV checklist before rostering [8].
18. **Worker classification record** per employee (`worker` / `non_worker` / `uncertain → treated as worker`) using the pack's `wrk_is_worker()` logic, reviewed annually and on role change; it drives leave floor, overtime, standing-order and dispute routing.

### D. Attendance and work measurement that respects privacy

19. **Lawful basis register:** attendance, payroll and statutory records = DPDP s.7(i) employment purpose (plus legal obligation); security logs = s.7(i) safeguarding from loss; anything else (wellbeing surveys, optional productivity insights) = consent, separately withdrawable. Every telemetry source is listed with purpose, retention and access roles in the employee privacy notice (s.5 format, available in English and Hindi; Eighth-Schedule languages on request) [45].
20. **Attendance capture (default ON):** login/logoff to Microsoft 365 / the Equator app, manual check-in with optional location *at check-in only* (coarse, city level), regularisation workflow with manager approval, and employee self-view of their own records. Biometric attendance OFF by default; if enabled for an office, store templates only on the device/vendor with SPDI-grade consent and never in HRMS.
21. **Work measurement (default ON):** output metrics only — cases handled, SLA adherence, tickets resolved, QA scores, goals/OKRs. Dashboards show team aggregates to managers; individual metrics visible to the employee and their direct manager only.
22. **Locked OFF (cannot be enabled by tenant admins):** keystroke logging, webcam capture or face-presence detection, covert screen recording, off-hours location tracking, reading personal email/messages, BYOD personal-area access. **Governed (Super-admin + DPO + counsel approval, time-boxed ≤ 30 days, per named employee, logged in the audit trail and disclosed to the employee after closure unless it would prejudice an investigation):** screenshots or screen recording for a specific investigation or regulated-data role.
23. **Retention:** raw login/session telemetry 1 year (DPDP Rule 8(3) floor) then aggregated to daily attendance; attendance registers 5 years after last entry (CoW r.51); investigation captures deleted at case closure + 1 year unless under legal hold. Monitoring data never feeds AI performance ratings without human review, and is excluded from POSH and health-sealed stores.
24. **No adverse action on telemetry alone:** any disciplinary step relying on monitoring data requires a human-reviewed explanation opportunity (MSO inquiry norms [12]).

### E. Anti-fraud control set for payroll

25. **Maker-checker everywhere money or identity changes:** employee master create/terminate, salary revision, bank details, component templates, parameter versions, off-cycle payments, F&F, loans/advances, reimbursements above a threshold. Maker ≠ checker enforced by user ID and by role; checker cannot be in the maker's reporting line for self-related records; no one can approve their own record or that of a relative (declared relationships from the COI register).
26. **Bank-account change protocol:** change only via the employee's own authenticated session (MFA) or HR-initiated with the employee's in-app confirmation; penny-drop/name-match verification against the account holder name; **cooling period of 3 working days** (configurable 1–7) before the new account is used for payment, with notification to the employee's registered email and phone and to the old account's last-used channel; changes requested within 5 days of a pay run are deferred to the next run unless the CFO approves; a reversal link in the notification freezes the change.
27. **Ghost-employee detection (weekly job + every run):** employees paid with no login/attendance in the period; duplicate PAN, Aadhaar-reference, UAN, ESI IP number, bank account, phone or email across employees; bank accounts shared with vendors or other employees; employees without a signed appointment letter (OSH s.6(1)(f)) or without onboarding evidence; employees whose manager is also their approver for every change; salary paid after the separation date; new hires added and paid in the same run. Each hit opens an investigation ticket; the run cannot be finalised with unresolved critical hits.
28. **Pay-run controls:** variance report vs previous run (headcount, gross, net, per-employee change > 10%), dual approval (HR then Finance), bank file generated only from the approved run, bank file hash recorded and checked against the bank's acknowledgement, payment by bank transfer only (CoW s.15), no manual edit of bank files.
29. **Statutory-dues integrity:** EPF/ESI/PT/LWF/TDS challans reconciled to the run; liability ageing alert before due dates; no settlement or F&F can waive statutory dues (pack HB `WRK_STATUTORY_DUES_WAIVER`).
30. **Audit trail:** append-only, hash-chained log of every payroll event with before/after values; exported in the portable data folders as Excel registers plus PDF summaries; available to internal/external auditors via read-only roles.
31. **Segregation for group operations:** cross-functional staff who work for several Infrabyx companies are employed by one legal employer per contract; time/cost allocations to other group entities are inter-company charges, not second payrolls; one PAN cannot have two active employments within the group without an explicit secondment record.

### F. Chambers' HR & payroll module (small-establishment mode)

32. **Size-aware compliance wizard:** at setup the chamber enters headcount by class (employees, fixed-term, contract staff, junior advocates on retainer, consultants); the engine switches on duties by threshold (ESI 10, gratuity 10, POSH IC 10 or Local Committee notice, OSH registration 10, EPF 20, bonus 20, S&E registration per State) and re-evaluates monthly on actual headcount.
33. **Engagement types:** `employee`, `fixed_term_employee` (IR Code s.2(o) parity; gratuity after 1 year), `apprentice`, `professional_engagement` (advocates, consultants — invoice/fee note, TDS s.393(1) Sl. 6(iii) at 10% above ₹50,000 per tax year, Form 131 certificates), `contractor_staff` (via agency; principal-employer checks on wages paid). A misclassification checklist (control, hours, exclusivity, tools, integration) warns before a `professional_engagement` is created for a role that looks like employment.
34. **Defaults:** monthly pay calendar; appointment letter template (OSH format) for every hire; bank-only payment; wage slips; leave template "statutory minimum" vs "Equator standard"; registers generated automatically.

### G. Governance and what stays OFF pending counsel / CA

35. **OFF pending opinion:** PT and LWF on remote employees' residence State (CA); EPF September 2026 split-month method (EPFO guidance); bonus eligibility/calculation ceilings (until notified); Delhi S&E registration status; Maharashtra 2025 ordinance status; Rajasthan exemption below 10; whether Equator Marketplace pay-outs make Equator an "aggregator" under CoSS (counsel); any screen-capture feature (DPO + counsel).
36. **Locked (tenant cannot change):** s.2(y) classification logic; 50% deduction cap; 2× overtime floor; statutory clocks; national holidays; prohibition list in item 22; maker-checker on bank changes; statutory-dues non-waiver.
37. **Configurable by tenant within limits:** leave policy above floor; holiday festival choices; cooling-period length (1–7 days); variance thresholds; pay date (any date up to the statutory deadline); salary structure templates (within classes).
