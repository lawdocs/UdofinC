#### 1.4 IMI status — is the pack's existing finding still accurate?

- IMI published "New IMI Certified Mediator Training Program — Equa.Law" on 1 June 2024 (page modified 14 Aug 2024): Equa.Law (trainings.equa.law) is approved as an IMI **Certified Mediator Training Program (CMTP)** delivered online; "40-hour IMI-certified Mediation Training" with optional country modules; graduates are eligible to **apply** to become IMI Qualified Mediators; the page is tagged "RMTP" [20] [V]. An IMI-hosted PDF titled "imi cmtp equa law" also appears in search results [21] (not opened).
- A WebSearch of imimediation.org for an Equa/Equa.Law QAP returned no QAP listing for Equa (other QAPs such as ADRC, Concilia, MII and IFCM appear) [22] — absence, not proof [U].
- The home page says "The IMI Accredited and Certified Mediation Training Program" and shows a "Qualifying Assessment Program" heading [1]; the 40-hour product page itself does not mention IMI and instead says "various dispute resolution platforms have accredited this training, especially Equa.Law" [5].
- **Conclusion:** the pack's finding (Appendix N §9 and Appendix T §3.3–3.4) remains **accurate as of 23 Oct 2026**: the site is run as Equa Trainings / Equa.Law by Infrabyx Consultancy (OPC) Pvt Ltd; IMI recognition is a recognised training programme (CMTP, now RMTP tier), **not a QAP**. Corrections C1–C11 in Appendix N remain outstanding — every one was still visible today. **New items for the corrections list [R]:** (C12) "ISO27001:2018" and GDPR/CCPA/HIPAA/SOC 2 claims on About Us — evidence or remove; (C13) "company registered under the Companies Act, 2013" applied to a brand — replace with the legal entity name, CIN and GSTIN; (C14) personal Gmail behind the mailto link; (C15) inconsistent comparison prices ($700 vs $608.63) — CCPA reference-price risk; (C16) Lorem Ipsum and "coming soon" pages linked from the footer; (C17) empty syllabus and off-topic "industry-ready" block on the flagship course; (C18) USD-only display with no GST statement for Indian consumers.

#### 1.5 How training could feed the Equator marketplace and neutral pipeline

Pipeline today: none — the site has no link to Equator, no panel/empanelment pathway, and no graduate directory [1][5][17]. Opportunities and limits [R]:

1. **Neutral funnel (legitimate):** a learner who completes any recognised 40-hour course (the Institute's or another provider's) is invited to apply for empanelment and EQAP — already designed in Appendix N §9 (≥ 3 providers, no conditionality, no fast-track advantage for the Institute). The Institute is one source among several.
2. **Private-practice funnel:** graduates who want their own practice are natural CH-LITE/CH-SOLO subscribers (Appendix O §2.8). This is a SaaS sale, not an appointment, and does not touch neutrality — provided the Institute never implies that buying Chambers or a course leads to Equator work.
3. **CPD funnel:** specialisations (consumer, property, workplace, commercial, family, healthcare & insurance) map almost one-to-one to Equator verticals (FIN, COM, FAM, INS, WRK). Equator's **endorsement** catalogue (Appendix N) can accept CPD from any provider; the Institute's specialisations can be listed as one option.
4. **Student funnel:** "Trainings for students" (heading only) can feed Equator's Student Associate membership (₹0) and moot/competition events — sponsorship of student competitions is the only sponsorship Appendix O X-10 allows.
5. **Train-the-trainer / coach programme** is a supply-side engine for the Institute (more cohorts) and could later supply Equator's assessor roster only through Equator's own calibration (Appendix N §4) — never automatically.
6. **What must not happen:** training sold as a route to appointments; Institute-first ordering; learner data flowing into Equator case systems; Equator commissions on course sales without s.188 related-party approval (Appendix O §2.10).

#### 3.2 Guardrails used by professional marketplaces and regulators

1. **Payment for recommendation = ethics breach for lawyers.** NYSBA Ethics Opinion 1132 (Aug 2017) held that the per-service "marketing fee" Avvo deducted from lawyers' fees was an improper payment for a recommendation, because Avvo's ratings, eligibility rules and satisfaction guarantee implied it recommended its lawyers; a June 2017 New Jersey joint opinion found it an impermissible referral fee and fee-sharing; Ohio, South Carolina and Pennsylvania had earlier decisions [45] [S]. The Indian analogue is stricter: Rule 36 BCI Rules bars direct or indirect solicitation and advertising; the 2008 proviso allows websites only with name, address, contact details, qualifications and areas of practice, with BCI intimation/approval [50][51] [S].
2. **Indian enforcement against directories.** Madras High Court, *P.N. Vignesh v Chairman and Members of the Bar Council* (judgment 3 Jul 2024; 2024 SCC OnLine Mad 2770) found online platforms labelling advocates "top" or "premium" and rejected the "mere directory" defence; BCI issued cease-and-desist notices to Quikr, Sulekha, Just Dial and Grotal (6–7 Jul 2024) and directed State Bar Councils to act and remove content within four weeks [48][50] [S]. The Supreme Court issued notice to BCI in *Sulekha.com New Media Pvt Ltd v P.N. Vignesh* (SLP (C) 25794/2024; report 13 Nov 2024) on whether aggregating advocate profiles with only BCI-permitted information violates Rule 36; no stay was reported [49] [S]. **Status in Oct 2026: not confirmed** [U].
3. **Ranking transparency and sponsored labels.** Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 r.5 requires marketplaces to display seller details and the main parameters that determine ranking; r.4 requires a grievance officer (48-hour acknowledgement, one-month redress) and forbids pre-ticked consent [52] [S]. A law-firm summary reports the **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Amendment Rules 2026** (notified 9 Sep 2026, CG-DL-E-10092026-276125) adding: clear and prominent labelling of sponsored listings; publication of ranking parameters in descending order of importance with relative weight (r.5(3)(f)); a ban on search manipulation (r.4(11)(c)); prior price shown on price reductions (lowest price in the previous 30 days); annual dark-pattern self-audit with a displayed compliance certificate; NCH convergence; seller government identifiers such as GSTIN (r.6(5)(j)) [53] [S — Gazette text not opened; confirm].
4. **Dark patterns.** CCPA Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns 2023 (30 Nov 2023) list 13 patterns: false urgency, basket sneaking, confirm shaming, forced action, subscription trap, interface interference, bait and switch, drip pricing, disguised advertisement, nagging, trick question, SaaS billing, rogue malware; they apply to platforms, advertisers and sellers [46] [S]. Appendix O §6 already maps DP-01…DP-13 [43].
5. **Misleading advertisements.** CCPA Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements 2022 (9 Jun 2022): claims must be truthful and substantiated; bait ads only with adequate supply and disclosed limits; "free" only if the consumer pays nothing beyond unavoidable delivery cost; endorsers must do due diligence and disclose material connections; disclaimers cannot contradict or hide the main claim [47] [S]. CPA 2019 penalties: up to ₹10 lakh (first) / ₹50 lakh (subsequent) for false or misleading ads causing harm, as summarised in [46][47].
6. **Credential-first directories.** CMC lists only Regulated Mediators meeting training, observation, CPD, insurance and complaints standards [34]; Mediate.com gates Featured on 40 h training and 10 cases but still sells placement [31]. The institutional model (CMC, SIAC panel, IIAC Chamber) ties listing to standards, not payment.
7. **Outcome-neutral commissions.** Where institutions take a percentage of neutral fees (LCIA 5%, JAMS 13%), the rate is published, uniform and institution-controlled [27][28]. Tiered take-rates that fall with volume (Jupitice per [42]) give the platform an incentive to steer high-value work — avoid for administered work.

## Design implications for Equator

All items are **[R]** (recommendations) unless they restate a pack rule. They extend, and do not override, Appendix O RA-1…RA-7 and deny-list X-01…X-16; where v3.0 management requests (switchable streams, paid "preference" placement, advertisements) touch a deny-list item, the item stays locked for administered work and the new stream is built **OFF** behind a governed switch.

### A. Switchable revenue-stream engine (build in P05 / Ops Platform)

1. **Revenue Stream Registry** `billing.revenue_streams` (one row per stream in catalogue C below): `code`, `name`, `stream_type` (one-time, subscription, rental, usage/PAYG, transactional, commission, membership, advertising, sponsorship, services), `payer_segment[]`, `pricing_unit`, `cadence`, `revenue_owner` (Equator / neutral pass-through / Institute / group company), `sac_code`, `gst_treatment`, `legal_dependencies[]` (FK to Appendix G Legal Rules Register), `neutrality_class` (`none` | `disclose` | `separate_surface_only` | `prohibited_in_administered`), `requires_board_resolution` (bool), `requires_counsel_opinion` (bool), `status` (`draft → legal_review → board_approved → enabled → suspended → retired`), `enabled_scopes` (tenant types, verticals, geographies), `kill_switch` (bool), `effective_from/to`, `version`.
2. **State changes are maker-checker**: maker (`revenue_manager`) proposes; checker (`cfo` or delegate) approves; streams flagged `requires_counsel_opinion` cannot reach `enabled` without an attached opinion record, and streams flagged `requires_board_resolution` without a Board resolution reference (resolution number, date). Every transition emits `equator.billing.stream.changed.v1` to the hash-chained audit log.
3. **Tenant self-service, not super-admin**: a Chambers/Institution/Enterprise tenant may enable or disable, within its own tenant, only streams the registry marks `tenant_configurable = true` (e.g., its own fee schedule, own membership, own events); price fields are bounded by registry min/max and by Appendix B where B governs.
4. **Locked streams**: rows with `neutrality_class = prohibited_in_administered` can never be enabled for `case_scope = 'administered'`; the `revenue-denylist` CI job (Appendix O §1.5) is extended to read the registry and fail if any enabled stream maps to X-01…X-16 or to the new X-17…X-20 below.
5. **New deny-list items**: **X-17** pay-per-lead or pay-per-enquiry fees charged to advocates (BCI r.36; NYSBA Op. 1132 reasoning) [45][50]; **X-18** paid labels such as "top", "premium", "best", "recommended" on any person in any directory (*P.N. Vignesh*, Madras HC 2024) [48][50]; **X-19** platform take-rate that falls as a neutral's billed fees rise, on any Equator-originated work (steering incentive) [42]; **X-20** marketing use of any data from a case, grievance, POSH or HR record (DPDP purpose limitation; NN-16).
6. **Kill switch** per stream, effective within 5 minutes, that stops new charges, hides CMS price blocks and CTAs, and keeps existing obligations (run-off) honoured.
7. **Reporting**: finance dashboard (Appendix O §1.4) adds stream-level revenue, cost-to-serve, contribution margin and a **neutrality-exposure tile** (share of revenue from `disclose` and `separate_surface_only` streams).

### B. "Preference" placement for private-practice neutrals — how to build it without breaking neutrality (default OFF)

8. Two physically separate surfaces: (a) the **Institutional Panel Directory and allocation engine** (Appendix N) — never reads billing data, seeded-random order, no paid markers (RA-4, X-03, unchanged); (b) an optional **Private Practice Marketplace** where parties choose and engage a neutral directly for a non-administered matter (Chambers bookings).
9. Sponsored placement may exist only on surface (b), behind flag `marketplace.sponsored_placement` = **OFF**, gated on: counsel opinion on BCI r.36 and the outcome of *Sulekha v P.N. Vignesh* in the Supreme Court [49]; E-Commerce Rules 2020 as amended in 2026 (sponsored labelling, ranking-parameter disclosure) [52][53]; Dark Patterns Guidelines (disguised advertisement) [46]; Board resolution.
10. If enabled: label "Sponsored" on every placement, same font size as the name, adjacent to it; maximum 2 sponsored slots per results page, visually separated above organic results; organic ranking parameters published in descending order of weight on `/marketplace/how-ranking-works`; no sponsored marker on profiles; no sponsored slot for a neutral with an open Equator-administered matter involving the searching party; **advocate-neutrals excluded** until counsel clears r.36 (retired judges and non-advocate professionals only); credential floor (40 h training + Equator code acceptance + no open discipline) like Mediate.com Featured eligibility [31]; pro-rata refund on removal; monthly cap on spend; annual dark-pattern self-audit artefact [53].
11. Product name in all copy: "Sponsored listing" — never "preference", "priority", "premium" or "featured" (X-18).
12. CI test `NO_PAID_PLACEMENT_INSTITUTIONAL` (existing) extended with `SPONSORED_LABEL_PRESENT` and `RANKING_PARAMS_PUBLISHED` for surface (b).
