## 1. Equa Institutional Rules ("Equa Dispute Resolution Rules")

URL: https://sites.google.com/view/equalaw/rules-procedure/equa-institutional-rules
Status: Loaded in full. The rules sit entirely on this page; no linked PDF or Google Doc.

**Preamble.** Equa is a virtual dispute resolution centre that gives quick, easy access to resolve complaints against companies and individuals, and gives businesses a way to maintain good customer relations.

### Part I — General

**1. Scope of Application**
- 1.1 Title: "Equa Dispute Resolution Rules" (the "Rules").
- 1.2 Intended for Equa's online dispute resolution (ODR) framework.
- 1.3 Govern proceedings on the Equa platform (www.equa.law) where (a) the parties' pre-existing agreement contains an Equa dispute resolution clause; or (b) one party approaches Equa, Equa sends the other party an invitation, and the invitation is accepted.
- 1.4 Proceedings begin when the parties accept the Terms and Conditions of participation.
- 1.5 Part II applies where the parties agreed to refer disputes to Equa for arbitration.
- 1.6 Parts III and IV apply where the parties agreed to mediation and negotiation, respectively.

**2. Definitions** (2.1: terms have these meanings unless context requires otherwise)
- (a) **Communication** — any communication (statement, declaration, demand, notice, response, submission, proposal, notification or request) made through information generated, uploaded, conducted and transmitted on the Equa Platform.
- (b) **Neutral** — the mediator or arbitrator appointed to conduct the Proceedings at the relevant time.
- (c) **Case Manager** — Equa's representative who coordinates and facilitates the parties in the proceedings.
- (d) **Online Dispute Resolution (ODR)** — a mechanism for resolving disputes using electronic communications and other ICT.
- **Equa** — a platform created as a cost-effective ODR framework offering quick, seamless, technology-assisted dispute resolution, intended to help disputes be enforced across legal jurisdictions.
- **Arbitral Award** — an award "rendered by Equa", described as enforceable under the New York Convention (worldwide) and India's Arbitration and Conciliation Act.
- Site defect: "Proceedings" is capitalised throughout but never defined. The definition of "Arbitral Award" attributes the award to Equa, not to the tribunal.

**3. Communication**
- 3.1 Communications before a Case Manager is assigned go to **registrar@equa.law**.
- 3.2 Unless the Rules provide otherwise, all communications during proceedings go to Equa and are uploaded and transmitted through the online platform.
- 3.3 Equa promptly acknowledges receipt of any Communication from a party or the Neutral at their electronic addresses.
- 3.4 By using the platform, a party agrees that electronic transmission through it is valid service.
- 3.5 Rule 3.4 extends to other agreed channels, e.g. **WhatsApp or Telegram**.
- 3.6 A platform communication is deemed received on the day it is dispatched.
- 3.7 Equa promptly notifies all parties and the Neutral when the negotiation, mediation and arbitration stages begin and end.
- 3.8 Time periods run from the day after receipt under Rule 3.6.

### Part II — Arbitration
**4. Dispute Resolve Process through Arbitration**
- 4.1 The intended claimant may first use the **Dispute Explorer** to learn their rights and remedies, then upload documents describing the dispute.
- 4.2 After the claimant gathers documents and evidence, the system suggests the best method to manage the dispute.
- 4.3 If the claimant agrees, they use the **"File a Claim/Proceed and Resolve"** link, and proceedings begin.
- 4.4 For queries, the claimant may contact the Equa administrator or another authorised person.

### Part III — Mediation
**5. Dispute Resolve Process through Mediation** — Rules 5.1–5.4 mirror Rules 4.1–4.4, applied to mediation.

### Part IV — Negotiation
**6. Dispute Resolve Process through Negotiation** — Rules 6.1–6.4 mirror Rules 4.1–4.4, applied to negotiation.

### "Part IV" — Procedural Rules (site reuses the Part IV label)
**7. Pre-Dispute Consultation**
- 7.1 Within **7 days** of the dispute being referred to Equa, the ODR platform sends a notification with details of the pre-dispute consultation meeting and the assigned case manager (the sentence is cut off on the site after "assigned to the").
- 7.2 The meeting gives an overview of the process and of platform use, and answers parties' questions.
- 7.3 The Case Manager handles administrative, secretarial and Equa-related queries for the life of the case.

**8. Appointment of Neutral**
- 8.1 Where the parties chose arbitration or mediation through Equa, one neutral is selected from Equa's list of qualified neutrals.
- 8.2 Equa promptly notifies the parties of the neutral's name and identifying information.
- 8.3 Arbitration: unless otherwise agreed, a **sole arbitrator appointed by Equa**. For a **three-member tribunal**, claimants jointly nominate one, respondents jointly nominate one, and the two nominees jointly choose the presiding arbitrator.
- 8.4 Equa considers any qualifications the parties requested.
- 8.5 The neutral declares independence and discloses circumstances that could give justifiable doubts about impartiality or independence; the ODR provider may, at its discretion, decline to appoint.
- 8.6 On appointment, the ODR provider notifies the parties.
- 8.7 Equa obtains declarations from the parties that they have no objection to the appointment.
- 8.8 By accepting, the neutral confirms they can devote the time needed to conduct proceedings diligently, efficiently and within the Rules' time limits.
- 8.9 On accepting, the neutral makes a declaration of impartiality and independence substantially in a prescribed form ("e-declaration to be made available" — form not published).

**9. Challenge to Neutrals**
- 9.1 Either party may object to the neutral within **7 days**: (a) of notice of appointment, without giving reasons (not available where the appointment followed the parties' agreed choice); or (b) of learning of a fact likely to give justifiable doubts about impartiality or independence, with reasons given to the Case Manager at any point during the proceedings.
- Site gap: no procedure for deciding a challenge or substituting the neutral.

### "Part IV" — Miscellaneous
**10. Confidentiality Clause**
- 10.1 Unless the parties agree otherwise, and subject to applicable law, all communications made with or exchanged through Equa are confidential.
- 10.2 All users must follow Equa's Terms and Conditions (confidentiality requirements and privacy policies) and **digitally sign** them to show consent.
- 10.3 Everyone involved (dispute administrators, case managers, parties, representatives, arbitrators and mediators, factual and expert witnesses) must sign an **NDA/confidentiality agreement** binding them to Equa's confidentiality terms.
- 10.4 Reads: "Whether we should publish an award or not? General Provision." — an internal drafting note left on the live page, not an operative rule.

**11. Costs Clause** — "Costs" includes: (i) fees of arbitrators or mediators; (ii) experts appointed by the tribunal to evaluate evidence or for any other purpose the arbitrator thinks fit; (iii) legal and other costs incurred during the arbitral or mediation proceedings; (iv) the **case management fee charged by Equa**.

**12. Data Protection**
- Equa's processing of personal data is subject to applicable data protection law; its data protection notice is on the website.
- Early in an arbitration, the tribunal (consulting the parties and, where appropriate, Equa) considers adopting (i) information security measures for physical and electronic information shared, and (ii) measures for handling personal data produced or exchanged.
- Equa and the tribunal may issue information security / data protection directions, binding on the parties and (when issued by Equa) on tribunal members, subject to mandatory law.

**13. Alteration of Costs** — Costs are "prevalently" shared equally or borne by the unsuccessful party/parties; "Equa Reserves/Arbitrator reserves" the right to apportion costs as it thinks fit on the facts (who holds the power is left unresolved on the site).

**14. Legal Representative** — Parties may appoint representatives of their choice and must state, in the Statement of Claim or Defence, the representative's name, contact details and authority to act.

**15. Allocation of Costs – "Decide"** (heading shows the choice was never made) — Option 1: equal sharing, parties jointly and severally liable for Clause 11 fees and expenses. Option 2: the losing party bears costs and indemnifies the successful party.

**16. Exclusion of Liability**
- 16.1 Parties waive, to the fullest extent permitted under the **Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996**, any claim against Equa, its agents and employees for any act or omission in or connected to ODR proceedings.
- 16.2 Waiver excludes "international wrongdoings" (likely "intentional") and fraud by Equa, its agents, employees or neutrals.
- 16.3 Comments by neutrals, Equa, its agents or employees in or connected to proceedings are not to be treated as denunciatory and cannot found defamation, libel, slander or similar complaints.

**17. Seat of Dispute Resolution** — Unless the parties agree otherwise, or the facts and circumstances warrant otherwise, the seat is **New Delhi, India**.

Site gaps in the Rules: no Statement of Claim/Defence contents or timelines; no hearing, evidence, interim measures, award time limit (cf. s.29A A&C Act), award form, correction or termination provisions; no mediation settlement / s.30 settlement-award mechanics; no language clause; no governing law clause; no fee amounts (cross-refers to Schedule of Fee).

## 2. Neutrals' Code of Professional Conduct

URL: https://sites.google.com/view/equalaw/neutrals/code-of-professional-conduct
Status: Loaded in full; no truncation. No linked PDF/Doc. Referenced but not published: Agreement to Mediate, Equa Feedback Request Form, Feedbook, Equa Profile, Reviewer, Equa Professional Conduct Assessment Process (all undefined on the site).

**Intro.** A short statement of the ethical standards users of mediation services can expect from **Equa Certified Neutrals**.

**1. Neutral's Appointment**
- 1.1 Use of title and logo — a neutral who ceases to meet Equa certification requirements loses the right to use the title "Equa Certified Neutral" and the Equa name/logo; their Profile is removed from the Equa.Law website.
- 1.2 Promotion of services — subject to applicable law and professional regulation, neutrals must present their practice truthfully and accurately; may quote from/link to their Equa Profile and copy it or extracts for their own professional purposes.
- 1.3.1 Before mediation begins, the neutral tells the parties their relevant background and experience.
- 1.3.2 The neutral tells the parties they will be invited to give feedback at any stage, including written feedback at the end of the ADR proceedings.
- 1.3.3 Before appointment, the neutral makes reasonable inquiries into any relationship or interest in the subject matter that could create a real or perceived conflict, discloses it and obtains the parties' consent to continue. Even with consent, the neutral must decline if they believe the relationship/interest threatens their ability to act impartially.

**2. Diligence** — Neutrals may accept arbitrator or mediator assignments where competent; they should have the time, energy and procedural and subject-matter expertise to meet the parties' reasonable expectations.

**3. Impartiality**
- 3.1 Conduct impartially; avoid bias arising from reactions to a participant's personal characteristics, background or values; the neutral's personal, professional or financial interest in the subject matter; or pre-existing relationships. If unable to remain impartial, say so and withdraw.
- 3.2 Before accepting, disclose anything known that may, or may appear to, materially affect impartiality; the duty continues throughout.
- 3.3 Such relationships/interests do not automatically disqualify, if fully disclosed and resolved to the satisfaction of the parties and the neutral.
- 3.4 Disclosure is ongoing; newly discovered interests/relationships must be disclosed and the parties must renew consent.
- 3.5 If any party objects after disclosure, the neutral must withdraw.
- 3.6 From acceptance until the process ends, no financial, business, professional, family or social relationships, or financial/personal interests, likely to create an actual or perceived threat to impartiality. For perceived threats only, may proceed after full disclosure and party consent.
- 3.7 **Cooling-off: 12 months** after the proceedings end, the neutral must not act in an advisory capacity for, or accept employment with, any party in the same or a substantially related matter, unless all parties expressly consent after full disclosure. Serving as neutral in other proceedings involving the parties is not advisory representation.

**4. Mediation Process**
- 4.1 Procedure — ensure parties and advisers understand the process, their roles, the neutral's role and the enforceability of any agreement; parties must understand and agree to the governing terms (including confidentiality) before mediation starts. Best practice: a written **Agreement to Mediate**, unless parties/circumstances dictate otherwise.
- 4.2.1 Explain the process and confirm consent to it and to the selected neutral (subject to any law, court rule or contract requiring a particular process/neutral). All parties must know they have an equal opportunity for private pre-mediation communications with the neutral.
- 4.2.2 Procedural fairness — adequate opportunity to be heard, participate, and obtain legal or other counsel before any resolution is finalised.
- 4.2.3 Take reasonable steps to prevent misconduct that could invalidate an agreement or create/worsen a hostile environment; agreements must be voluntary and with knowing consent.
- 4.3.1 Parties may withdraw at any time by informing the neutral, unless law, court rules or contract provide otherwise.
- 4.3.2 Neutral must withdraw if negotiations appear headed to an unconscionable or illegal outcome (unconscionable = result of undue pressure, exploitation or duress, where one party exploits a power imbalance so the agreement "shocks the conscience" and violates accepted legal and cultural norms of fairness).
- 4.4 Feedback — unless inappropriate, at the end of a proceeding invite parties, advisers and co-neutrals to complete an **Equa Feedback Request Form**, returned to the neutral or to the **Reviewer** named in their Equa Profile; used to prepare the neutral's **Feedbook**.
- 4.5.1 Fees — before accepting, agree with the parties how fees and expenses are calculated and paid and, if shared, in what proportions. A neutral who withdraws must refund fees paid for the period after withdrawal.
- 4.5.2 No suggestion that remuneration depends on or relates to the outcome.

**5. Confidentiality**
- 5.1.1 Keep confidential all information acquired while serving, except: (i) when compelled by law or by a government agency with authority and jurisdiction; (ii) disclosure under para 5.1, where recipients must also keep it confidential; (iii) information that enters the public domain other than through the neutral; (iv) when released by the parties, or as necessary to defend the neutral against proceedings/charges risking their liability; (v) to prevent death, imminent bodily harm or severe damage to an identifiable third party, or illegal and morally objectionable acts — unless law requires disclosure, first a good-faith effort to persuade the party/counsel/advisers to remedy the situation.
- 5.1.2 May disclose having previously mediated a case involving a party, but no details.
- 5.1.3 Discuss confidentiality with parties before or at the start, and obtain consent to any practice involving disclosure.
- 5.1.4 After the proceeding, never give evidence or testify for one party against another party from the same mediation where confidential information was acquired from that other party, unless no longer confidential or the protected party consents.

**6. Professional Conduct Issues and Complaints**
- 6.1 A certified neutral may consult their **Reviewer** on professional or ethical dilemmas.
- 6.2 A party who believes the Code was not followed may activate the **Equa Professional Conduct Assessment Process** (process not published).

**The Credits** — stated as consistent with: (1) CPR-Georgetown Commission on Ethics & Standards in ADR, Model Rule for the Lawyer as Third Party Neutral (2002); (2) UIA Forum of Mediation Centres, Code of Conduct for Mediators (2003); (3) European Commission, European Code of Conduct for Mediators (2004); (4) Model Standards of Conduct for Mediators (2005), adopted by AAA, ABA and ACR; (5) Law Council of Australia, Ethical Guidelines for Mediators (2006); (6) JAMS Mediators Ethical Guidelines; (7) Chartered Institute of Arbitrators guidelines on mediator appointment, confidentiality and termination; (8) Swiss Rules of Commercial Mediation.

Observations: The Code is mediation-centric (title says "Neutrals" but most clauses refer to mediation). No reference to the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 (s.12 / Fifth, Sixth and Seventh Schedules) or the Mediation Act 2023. Typo on site: "Nutral" (4.4).
